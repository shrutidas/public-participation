Case Study 1  ·  COVID-19 School Closures and Reopenings
Measured outcomes
Confidence in public schools

Gallup confidence fell from 41% in 2020 to 28% in 2022, concentrated among Republicans.

Public school enrollment

Down 1.4 million students between fall 2019 and fall 2020; not recovered.

Chronic absenteeism

About 22% of students missed a tenth or more of days through 2024-25.

Test scores

Fell sharply after the pandemic; the decline began years earlier.

Infections and deaths

To be added.

What happened
Public participation07/2020

Teachers' unions became the dominant local advocacy force. Union presence predicted remote starts across 250 large districts.

Public opinion07/2020

KFF found 60% of parents preferred opening later. Gallup found parents split 36-28-36 across in-person, remote, and hybrid.

★Parent surveys existed; nothing required districts to act on them
Policy09/2020

Reopening became decentralized and uneven. 48 states left modality decisions to local districts.

★School board public comment: advisory only
Events02/2021

San Francisco's city attorney sued the city's own school district to force a return to in-person instruction.

Knowledge05/2022

Remote instruction explained a measurable share of the widening gap between high- and low-poverty schools.

Public opinion08/2022

Gallup confidence in public schools fell to 28%, the lowest on record.

Knowledge01/2026

Teachers' trust with parents was significantly related to absence rates, more strongly after the pandemic.

A standing parent and community body can govern schools competently.
  • Most of Chicago's parent-majority school councils carried out their governing duties.Bryk et al., 2010
  • Chicago schools whose councils voted to remove police saw no worsening in discipline or climate.Chicago Consortium, 2022
  • Chicago Local School CouncilsChicago Public Schools
  • A fifth of the Chicago schools asked to vote on police had no council or no quorum.Chalkbeat, 2020
  • More than 1,400 Chicago council seats sat empty after an election.Chicago Sun-Times, 2022

Having to justify a decision to an outside body puts the evidence on the public record.
  • When residents used a legal records request instead of simply asking, councils handed over more information.Worthy et al., 2017
  • When central bankers learned their records would be published, their discussions leaned harder on data.Meade and Stasavage, 2008
  • The same bankers voiced less disagreement with the chair once transcripts were public.Meade and Stasavage, 2008
  • Agencies required to analyze major rules often produced weak analyses and rarely used them.Ellig, 2016
+ 1 more
3
A body that can say no holds decision makers to account.
  • When Kenyan parents on school committees got real say over locally hired teachers, fewer jobs went to relatives.Duflo, Dupas & Kremer, 2015
  • Where Swiss cantons required a public vote on major spending, governments spent less.Feld and Matsusaka, 2003
  • At larger scale, community oversight of clinics did not make citizens press harder or improve health.Raffler et al., 2020
  • Police departments with civilian oversight boards did no better on most measures.Ali and Pirog, 2019
+ 1 more, 1 more against
4
A binding role before decisions raises trust and engagement.
  • New Yorkers who took part in participatory budgeting became more likely to vote afterward.Johnson et al., 2021
  • Americans in states that vote on ballot measures felt more able to affect politics.Smith and Tolbert, 2004
  • Brazilian Municipal Participatory BudgetingGonçalves, 2014
  • States that leaned hardest on ballot measures ended up with weaker party organizations.Bowler and Donovan, 2006
  • People in states that use ballot measures trust their legislature less.Dyck, 2009

What the shapes mean. A filled target is an outcome the record measured. An empty target is an outcome a proposal aims at; it sits at the head of the implication and is drawn from the proposal by a green arrow. Numbered dark squares are the project's own claims. In the ledger under each claim, a filled dot is a finding for the claim, an outlined square is a case study, and a struck-through outline is a finding against. The tally strip under each claim is the count of for (green) and against (gray), readable at any zoom. Boxes remain only for the two things that are objects in the world: timeline events (now flat entries on a spine) and the proposal tag.
