# Proposed Public Participation: audit and rewrite of all 16 proposals

Prepared 2026-09-03. Every item below is applied in the data and the interface in commit form: the map card is one purple card (header once, then Mechanism, an optional one-sentence summary, and Effects it aims at), the "Conjectured, not measured" line is gone from the card and the sidebar, the sidebar's chain and effects lists are gone (the map carries both), and each proposal's title, mechanism label, description panel, and causal-link wording are the revised text shown here. The `impactsConjectured` field stays in the data for the research CSV; nothing renders it.

Mechanism labels use the governance vocabulary from the brief: public notice and comment, deliberative forum, standing advisory council, community representation, co-governance, binding participation right, citizen oversight, devolved local authority, locally accountable authority, intergovernmental coordination. "Confederal" no longer appears anywhere. Three proposals (the two open-data proposals and the evaluation condition) are transparency or accountability measures rather than participation rights; their labels and panels now say so instead of borrowing participation language.

## COVID-19 School Closures

### Let a randomly selected citizens' assembly decide how schools reopen

1. **Existing title:** Regional citizen assemblies that can decide school reopening
2. **Existing mechanism label:** Citizens Assembly (Sortition, Rotation, Confederal)
3. **Mechanism classification:** Deliberative forum with a binding participation right
4. **Diagnostic:** "Confederal" is misused for regions inside a state. "Sortition" and "Rotation" are member-selection rules, not the institutional device. The label never says whether the assembly decides or advises, which is the whole design.
5. **Revised unified-card title:** Let a randomly selected citizens' assembly decide how schools reopen
6. **Revised mechanism label:** Citizens' assembly with decision authority
7. **One-sentence summary for the card:** A state-convened assembly of randomly selected residents sets the fall instructional mode, and the state must adopt its decision or publicly explain why not.
8. **Revised description panel (125 words):** Give a randomly selected assembly of residents, convened by the state, authority to set the fall 2020 instructional mode for its region, within state public-health orders and civil-rights law. The state health department convenes the assembly in May 2020; members are drawn by lot from the region's residents, with seats rotating on a fixed schedule so no cohort becomes an incumbent class. Members hear from public-health, education, and building-safety experts, deliberate over several weeks, and vote. The decision binds districts unless the state publishes written reasons for overriding it, and the state must publish the evidence the assembly saw. Michigan created an appointed advisory council in May 2020 and seated a sortition panel only in November, after the decision it would have informed had passed.
9. **Rewritten causal links:**

   - Link 1. *Was:* "An assembly can be convened fast enough" — A sortition assembly can be convened and reach recommendations fast enough to decide how school would run in fall 2020.
     *Now:* "An assembly could be convened in time" — A randomly selected assembly could be convened and reach a decision between May and August 2020, in time to set how school runs that fall. This depends on a convening authority acting by May; the assemblies studied took two to five months.

   - Link 2. *Was:* "A binding remit could give non-union voices force" — A binding or quasi-binding remit could give parents and community members force comparable to collective bargaining, so the assembly's decision, not the bargained one, sets how school runs.
     *Now:* "A binding decision rule could give parents and community members force" — A binding or must-answer decision rule could give parents and community members weight comparable to collective bargaining, so the assembly's choice, not the bargained one, sets how school runs. No assembly has held this authority over a school decision.

   - Link 3. *Was:* "Assemblies raise perceived legitimacy" — An assembly process raises perceived legitimacy of the decision among the wider public.
     *Now:* "An assembly process may raise the decision's perceived legitimacy" — An assembly process may raise how legitimate the wider public finds the decision, where the assembly's recommendation is honored; the gains shrink or reverse when it is shelved.

   - Link 4. *Was:* "Legitimacy could hold enrollment, attendance, and confidence" — The assembly's decision could move through two channels: its choices about how school runs directly change remote exposure and its measured harms, and higher perceived legitimacy could reduce exit and absence and hold confidence in schools. The legitimacy channel is the unstudied one.
     *Now:* "Higher legitimacy could hold enrollment, attendance, and confidence" — The assembly's decision could act through two routes: its choice of instructional mode directly changes remote exposure and its measured harms, and higher perceived legitimacy could reduce exit and absence and hold confidence in schools. The legitimacy route is not tested in any school setting.

10. **Effects it aims at** (Public school enrollment; Chronic absenteeism; Confidence in public schools): All three effects are measured on the map. Enrollment and absenteeism connect through the mode choice and through legitimacy; confidence connects only through the legitimacy route, which is untested, so it is an appropriate aim rather than a claim.

### Convene parents, teachers, and experts to design safer ways to reopen

1. **Existing title:** Bring experts and the public together on safer reopening
2. **Existing mechanism label:** Minipublic with Mapped Expertise
3. **Mechanism classification:** Deliberative forum (advisory) with structured expert input
4. **Diagnostic:** "Minipublic" is specialist vocabulary. "Mapped expertise" describes the input, not the device. The label leaves open whether the forum advises or decides; it advises.
5. **Revised unified-card title:** Convene parents, teachers, and experts to design safer ways to reopen
6. **Revised mechanism label:** Deliberative forum with expert briefings
7. **One-sentence summary for the card:** A district-convened forum of residents and educators works with ventilation, epidemiology, and child-development experts to produce reopening options the board must answer.
8. **Revised description panel (134 words):** Give a district-convened forum of parents, teachers, and residents, briefed by ventilation, epidemiology, behavioral-science, and early-childhood experts, the standing to put reopening options before the school board, which must respond to each in writing. The district or county convenes the forum in April 2020, before the fall decision. Members are recruited to reflect the district's families, meet over four to eight weeks, and work from a written map of what each field knows and does not know. The forum's product is a ranked set of options, such as outdoor classes, ventilation upgrades, and returning the youngest grades first, with costs attached. The board keeps the decision; the forum is advisory, but its options and the board's reasons are published. In 2020, large urban districts chose between open and closed with no such option set.
9. **Rewritten causal links:**

   - Link 1. *Was:* "Experts with the public could widen the options" — Structured early engagement between mapped experts and the public could expand the options beyond open against closed.
     *Now:* "A forum with experts could widen the options beyond open or closed" — Structured early work between briefed experts and residents could put options on the table, such as outdoor classes, ventilation upgrades, and staged returns, that the open-or-closed debate of 2020 did not. No study tests this in a school-reopening setting.

   - Link 2. *Was:* "Ventilation and outdoor options make school safer" — Ventilation and outdoor options make in-person school materially safer.
     *Now:* "Ventilation and outdoor options make in-person school safer" — Mechanical ventilation and outdoor instruction lower in-school transmission risk; this link is directly measured.

   - Link 3. *Was:* "Safer options could change the decision" — Safer options, credibly communicated, could move the decision toward earlier in-person instruction.
     *Now:* "Safer options could move the decision toward in-person school" — Where the board finds the safer options credible and affordable, they could move the decision toward earlier in-person instruction. This depends on cost, trust in the forum, and the politics that in 2020 outweighed local case rates.

   - Link 4. *Was:* "More in-person school reduces measured harm" — More in-person instruction reduces learning loss, disenrollment, and later absence.
     *Now:* "More in-person school reduces measured harm" — More in-person instruction reduces learning loss, disenrollment, and later chronic absence; this link is directly measured across districts.

10. **Effects it aims at** (Test scores; Public school enrollment; Chronic absenteeism): Test scores, enrollment, and absenteeism are all measured on the map and all named in the final link. Appropriate.

### Let elected school boards decide reopening after a public deliberative step

1. **Existing title:** Let each district make its own reopening decision
2. **Existing mechanism label:** Confederal Decision-Making
3. **Mechanism classification:** Devolved local authority with a required deliberative step
4. **Diagnostic:** "Confederal" is wrong for districts inside a state. Decentralization alone is not public participation, and 48 states already delegated in 2020; the label must name the resident channel the proposal adds, a structured local resolution session and published outcome comparisons.
5. **Revised unified-card title:** Let elected school boards decide reopening after a public deliberative step
6. **Revised mechanism label:** Devolved local authority with public deliberation
7. **One-sentence summary for the card:** States hand the fall decision to elected school boards, require a facilitated public session before the vote, and publish outcome comparisons across districts.
8. **Revised description panel (123 words):** Give locally elected school boards authority to decide whether and how schools reopen, within statewide public-health guardrails, on condition that each board holds a facilitated public session with parents, staff, and health officials before it votes, and publishes its reasons. The state legislature or governor sets the delegation between June and August 2020, keeps statewide closure thresholds, and requires every district to report its instructional mode and case data to a public comparison. The public session is deliberative rather than binding: the board decides, but must answer the concerns raised. In 2020, 48 states left the choice to districts without a required resolution process or any organized comparison of results, and district decisions tracked partisanship and union strength more than local case rates.
9. **Rewritten causal links:**

   - Link 1. *Was:* "Decentralizing produces regional variation" — Decentralizing the decision produces regional variation in modality.
     *Now:* "Delegation to districts produces regional variation" — Delegating the decision to districts produces variation in instructional mode across regions; this happened in fall 2020 and is directly observed.

   - Link 2. *Was:* "Local decisions could track local conditions" — Decentralized decisions with a structured deliberative step could track local COVID conditions rather than politics.
     *Now:* "A deliberative step could make local decisions track local conditions" — A required public session before the vote could make district decisions track local COVID conditions rather than politics. In the observed delegation, without that step, decisions tracked partisanship and union strength; whether the step changes this is untested.

   - Link 3. *Was:* "Variation could generate learning in time to use it" — Regional variation could generate outcome comparisons that inform later decisions inside the decision window.
     *Now:* "Variation could produce comparisons in time to use them" — Published outcome comparisons across districts could inform later decisions inside the decision window. This depends on comparable data existing in time; no school-mode dataset existed until September 2021.

   - Link 4. *Was:* "Condition-tracking could cut remote exposure" — Where conditions allowed, staying open was compatible with limited transmission, so decisions that tracked conditions could have cut remote exposure and its measured harms.
     *Now:* "Decisions that track conditions could cut remote exposure" — Where conditions allowed, staying open was compatible with limited transmission, so decisions that tracked conditions could have reduced remote exposure and its measured harms.

10. **Effects it aims at** (Test scores; Public school enrollment): Test scores and enrollment are measured and reached only through reduced remote exposure in the final link. Appropriate. Chronic absenteeism rests on the same evidence and could be added.

### Publish local case, hospital, and school data from the first weeks

1. **Existing title:** Share local case and school data from the first weeks
2. **Existing mechanism label:** Open Data & Public Communication
3. **Mechanism classification:** Public data disclosure duty (transparency measure, not participation on its own)
4. **Diagnostic:** "Share" reads as voluntary, whereas the design is a duty on agencies to publish. The proposal defines no way for residents to act on the data, so it is a transparency device the other proposals depend on, and the label should say so.
5. **Revised unified-card title:** Publish local case, hospital, and school data from the first weeks
6. **Revised mechanism label:** Mandatory open data publication
7. **One-sentence summary for the card:** State health and education agencies must publish county-level case, hospital, and school-mode data weekly, with a plain statement of what is and is not known.
8. **Revised description panel (129 words):** Require state health departments, jointly with state education agencies, to publish county-level case, hospitalization, death, and school-operating-mode data every week from March 2020, with a plain statement of what is known and what is not. The duty falls on the agencies; the public's role is as reader, and any resident, journalist, or school board can use the same figures. Publication is mandatory and on a fixed schedule, and the agencies must publish their uncertainty and revise openly. Statewide health orders stay in place. This is a transparency measure rather than a participation right, and it sits on the map because the other proposals depend on it: in 2020 no federal school-mode dataset existed, the COVID-19 School Data Hub launched only in September 2021, and newspapers filled the county-data gap.
9. **Rewritten causal links:**

   - Link 1. *Was:* "Early local data publication is feasible" — Early local data publication is feasible and fills a real gap.
     *Now:* "Early local data publication is feasible and fills a real gap" — States could publish county and school-level data from the first weeks; a small grant-funded team later assembled school-mode data for 55,000 schools, and in 2020 no such dataset existed.

   - Link 2. *Was:* "Honest uncertainty does not cost trust" — Communicating data, including uncertainty, does not erode trust in the data or its source.
     *Now:* "Publishing uncertainty need not cost trust" — Publishing data with its uncertainty stated does not appear to reduce trust in the data or its source; the experiments come from news and survey settings, not school decisions.

   - Link 3. *Was:* "Shared data could head off polarization" — A shared local data layer could align risk perception across groups and head off polarization.
     *Now:* "Shared data could narrow the gap in how groups see the risk" — A shared local data layer could align risk perception across groups and slow polarization. This is contested: how the same facts were framed moved opinion by 17 points, so shared data alone may not do it.

   - Link 4. *Was:* "Shared understanding could hold confidence and enrollment" — Sustained shared understanding could hold confidence in the deciding institutions, reducing exit.
     *Now:* "Shared understanding could hold confidence and enrollment" — Sustained shared understanding could hold confidence in the deciding institutions and reduce exit. No study tests this route.

10. **Effects it aims at** (Confidence in public schools; Public school enrollment): Confidence and enrollment are measured on the map and reached only through the final, untested link. Acceptable as aims. Absenteeism is correctly not claimed.

### Give parents and community members a binding say before school closures

1. **Existing title:** Give parents a real say before closure decisions
2. **Existing mechanism label:** Stakeholder Participation, Standing Binding Channel
3. **Mechanism classification:** Standing council with a binding participation right (co-governance elements)
4. **Diagnostic:** "Stakeholder participation" is a vague term. "Channel" does not name the body. The seats (parents, educators, small business, public health, community leaders) and the binding element are the design and belong in the label.
5. **Revised unified-card title:** Give parents and community members a binding say before school closures
6. **Revised mechanism label:** Standing council with binding consultation
7. **One-sentence summary for the card:** A standing council of parents, educators, business owners, and health experts must be consulted before closure or reopening decisions, and its vote carries required consequences.
8. **Revised description panel (129 words):** Give a standing school- or district-level council, with seats for parents, educators, small business owners, public-health experts, and community leaders, a binding role in closure and reopening decisions, within state health orders and collective-bargaining law. Councils are elected or appointed by constituency before any crisis; the district must bring each closure decision to them, and a council vote either sets the decision or obliges the board to publish written reasons for overriding it. Participation is binding in that sense, not advisory. The council sees the same case data the district uses, and its votes are published. Chicago's Local School Councils show the form existed in statute in 2020; small business was the constituency the 2020 record left without any channel, while parents' participation relocated to lawsuits and recall campaigns.
9. **Rewritten causal links:**

   - Link 1. *Was:* "A standing parent body can govern competently" — A standing parent-community body with binding elements can govern schools competently at scale.
     *Now:* "A standing council can govern schools competently" — A standing parent-community body with binding elements can govern schools competently at scale; Chicago's councils are the measured case, with a minority showing serious shortcomings and quorum failures.

   - Link 2. *Was:* "Binding voice raises trust and engagement" — Upstream binding voice raises trust and downstream civic engagement.
     *Now:* "A binding role before decisions may raise trust and engagement" — Having a binding voice before decisions may raise trust in the institution and later civic engagement; the measured cases come from participatory budgeting and school councils, not closure decisions.

   - Link 3. *Was:* "Maintained trust could reduce exit and absence" — Maintained trust could reduce exit and absence.
     *Now:* "Maintained trust could reduce exit and absence" — Where trust holds, families may be less likely to leave the district or keep children home; this route is not tested in schools.

   - Link 4. *Was:* "Upstream voice could reduce lawsuits and recalls" — Binding upstream voice could reduce adversarial participation after decisions: recalls, litigation, and board conflict. This is the branch to the conjectured impact.
     *Now:* "A voice before decisions could reduce lawsuits and recalls" — A binding voice before decisions could reduce adversarial participation after them, such as recalls, litigation, and board conflict; the closest evidence comes from medical disclosure, not school governance.

10. **Effects it aims at** (Confidence in public schools; Public school enrollment; Chronic absenteeism): All three effects are measured on the map. Confidence connects through the trust link; enrollment and absenteeism through the exit-and-absence link. Appropriate.

## COVID-19 Vaccines

### Publish vaccine safety and case data early, with regular public briefings

1. **Existing title:** Share all vaccine and case data, early and openly
2. **Existing mechanism label:** Open Data & Public Briefings
3. **Mechanism classification:** Public data disclosure duty with recurring public briefings (transparency measure)
4. **Diagnostic:** Adequate but soft. "Share" reads as voluntary; the design is a commitment CDC binds itself to. Not participation on its own, which the panel should say.
5. **Revised unified-card title:** Publish vaccine safety and case data early, with regular public briefings
6. **Revised mechanism label:** Mandatory open data and briefings
7. **One-sentence summary for the card:** CDC commits to county-level open dashboards and recurring on-record briefings from the first week of the rollout, including unfavorable findings.
8. **Revised description panel (117 words):** Require CDC to publish county-level vaccination, case, hospitalization, and adverse-event data on open dashboards, and to hold recurring on-record public briefings, from December 2020 through the rollout, including findings unfavorable to the vaccines. The duty falls on the federal agency; states and the public are users of the data. Briefings run on a fixed schedule with questions taken, and the agency must state what it does not yet know. This is a transparency commitment rather than a participation right. In 2021 CDC held two telebriefings, newspapers filled the county-data vacuum, and booster and hospitalization data were withheld in 2021-22. The randomized evidence suggests openness about negatives raises trust in the authorities even when it lowers short-run acceptance.
9. **Rewritten causal links:**

   - Link 1. *Was:* "Openness sustains trust, with an acceptance dip" — Transparency and frequent briefing sustain institutional trust. The randomized evidence adds a complication: transparent negative communication raises trust while lowering short-run acceptance, so the chain's first two arrows can point in opposite directions.
     *Now:* "Openness may sustain trust, at the cost of a short-run acceptance dip" — Transparent, frequent briefing may sustain trust in health authorities. The randomized evidence adds a complication: openness about negative vaccine features raises trust while lowering short-run acceptance, so the first two arrows can pull in opposite directions.

   - Link 2. *Was:* "Trust raises vaccination" — Institutional trust raises uptake. The correlational record supports it; no causal test of raising institutional trust to raise uptake was identified, and the strongest US predictor of county uptake is partisan identity.
     *Now:* "Trust in authorities may raise vaccination" — Higher institutional trust is associated with higher uptake across countries. No causal test of raising trust to raise uptake was identified, and the strongest US county predictor is partisan identity.

   - Link 3. *Was:* "Vaccination prevents deaths" — Higher uptake prevents deaths among the unvaccinated.
     *Now:* "Vaccination prevents deaths" — Higher uptake prevents deaths among unvaccinated adults; this link is directly measured.

10. **Effects it aims at** (Deaths among unvaccinated adults): Deaths among unvaccinated adults is measured and connected through the final link. "Deaths and hospitalizations averted by vaccination" is also on the map and could be added on the same evidence.

### Convene a citizens' assembly before any vaccine mandate is imposed

1. **Existing title:** Public deliberation before any vaccine mandate
2. **Existing mechanism label:** Citizens Assembly, Minipublic
3. **Mechanism classification:** Deliberative forum (advisory) before a mandate decision
4. **Diagnostic:** The title is a noun phrase, not an action. "Minipublic" is jargon. The label does not say whether the assembly decides or advises; it advises and the decision-maker must respond.
5. **Revised unified-card title:** Convene a citizens' assembly before any vaccine mandate is imposed
6. **Revised mechanism label:** Deliberative forum before mandates
7. **One-sentence summary for the card:** Before ordering a mandate, the federal government or a city convenes a randomly selected assembly on the value tradeoff and must publicly answer its recommendation.
8. **Revised description panel (126 words):** Require the federal government, or a city considering its own mandate, to convene a randomly selected assembly of residents before imposing a vaccine mandate, with the assembly deciding the value question of whether and for whom to mandate, not the science, which is briefed to it. The convening authority keeps the final decision but must publish the assembly's recommendation and a written response before acting; participation is deliberative and advisory. Existing federal and state vaccination law and emergency-use rules stay in place, and regional variation is accepted: some places might choose not to mandate. Convened in June 2021 and reporting by late August, an assembly would have preceded Executive Order 14043 and the OSHA rule, both later cut down by the Senate and the Supreme Court.
9. **Rewritten causal links:**

   - Link 1. *Was:* "Deliberation raises the decision's legitimacy" — A mini-public convened before the decision raises its perceived legitimacy, especially among low-trust citizens.
     *Now:* "Deliberation before the decision may raise its perceived legitimacy" — A citizens' assembly convened before the decision may raise how legitimate the public finds it, especially among low-trust citizens; the effect is measured in survey experiments, not on a mandate.

   - Link 2. *Was:* "Legitimacy could improve compliance" — Perceived legitimacy could improve compliance with the mandate or reduce backlash.
     *Now:* "Higher legitimacy could improve compliance or reduce backlash" — Perceived legitimacy could improve compliance with a mandate or reduce backlash; the evidence comes from policing and lockdown compliance, not vaccination.

   - Link 3. *Was:* "A legitimate mandate could raise uptake" — A mandate carrying deliberative legitimacy could raise uptake at least as much as one imposed without it, with less backlash. The evidence below tests mandates alone; the deliberative increment is untested.
     *Now:* "A mandate with deliberative backing could raise uptake" — A mandate carrying deliberative legitimacy could raise uptake at least as much as one imposed without it, with less backlash. The evidence tests mandates alone, with contested results; the deliberative increment is untested.

   - Link 4. *Was:* "Uptake prevents deaths" — Higher uptake prevents deaths.
     *Now:* "Higher uptake prevents deaths" — Higher uptake prevents deaths; this link is directly measured.

10. **Effects it aims at** (Vaccination uptake after mandates; Deaths among unvaccinated adults): Uptake after mandates and deaths are both measured on the map and connected through the last two links. Appropriate.

### Let neighborhood task forces decide where and how vaccines are delivered

1. **Existing title:** Let communities design how vaccines reach them
2. **Existing mechanism label:** Community Advisory & Co-Design
3. **Mechanism classification:** Co-governance of delivery (community task forces with siting and scheduling authority)
4. **Diagnostic:** "Advisory" and "co-design" contradict each other. The Protect Chicago Plus model gave task forces real siting decisions, so co-governance is the accurate term.
5. **Revised unified-card title:** Let neighborhood task forces decide where and how vaccines are delivered
6. **Revised mechanism label:** Co-governance of vaccine delivery
7. **One-sentence summary for the card:** City health departments hand siting, hours, and outreach decisions for equity allocations to task forces of residents and local organizations.
8. **Revised description panel (118 words):** Give neighborhood task forces of residents, community organizations, clinics, and employers shared authority over where, when, and how vaccines are delivered in high-vulnerability zip codes: site selection, hours, mobile units, and paid-time-off coordination. The city or county health department keeps control of supply, eligibility rules, and safety standards, and commits a share of doses to the task forces' plans. Task forces form in February 2021, when scarce federal supply was being sited, and again in April 2021 when the constraint became access. Decisions are shared, not advisory, and the department publishes uptake by zip code. Protect Chicago Plus launched January 25, 2021 on this model; no study separates its co-governance component from the supply prioritization bundled with it.
9. **Rewritten causal links:**

   - Link 1. *Was:* "Co-design could put access where the barriers are" — Community co-design could land access infrastructure where the barriers actually are. The co-design component has not been isolated from supply prioritization in any study.
     *Now:* "Task forces could put access where the barriers are" — Community task forces could place access infrastructure where the barriers actually are. The co-governance component has not been isolated from supply prioritization in any study.

   - Link 2. *Was:* "Access raises uptake" — Access infrastructure raises uptake. The best-measured link in the case.
     *Now:* "Access raises uptake" — Access infrastructure raises uptake; the best-measured link in this case.

   - Link 3. *Was:* "Equitable uptake could narrow the gap earlier" — Equitable uptake could have narrowed the racial gap earlier than it narrowed in fact, cutting the never-estimated mortality cost of the spring 2021 lag.
     *Now:* "Earlier equitable uptake could narrow the racial gap sooner" — Equitable uptake could have narrowed the racial vaccination gap earlier than it narrowed in fact; the mortality cost of the spring 2021 lag has never been estimated.

   - Link 4. *Was:* "Uptake prevents deaths" — Higher and earlier uptake prevents deaths, including the never-estimated excess deaths from the spring 2021 lag.
     *Now:* "Higher and earlier uptake prevents deaths" — Higher and earlier uptake prevents deaths, including the unestimated excess deaths from the spring 2021 lag.

10. **Effects it aims at** (The racial vaccination gap; Deaths among unvaccinated adults): The racial vaccination gap and deaths are both measured on the map and connected through the last two links. Appropriate.

## GenX / PFAS on the Cape Fear

### Give plant workers a protected channel to report chemical hazards to regulators

1. **Existing title:** Give workers a protected way to report chemical risks
2. **Existing mechanism label:** Worker Committees & Protected Disclosure
3. **Mechanism classification:** Protected reporting channel with a duty on the regulator to act (worker oversight; binding participation right)
4. **Diagnostic:** "Committees" is not in the design. The device is a protected channel plus a legal duty on the regulator to receive, investigate, and publish.
5. **Revised unified-card title:** Give plant workers a protected channel to report chemical hazards to regulators
6. **Revised mechanism label:** Protected worker reporting channel
7. **One-sentence summary for the card:** Workers can report substantial-risk findings directly to EPA through a channel with anti-retaliation protection and a duty on the agency to act.
8. **Revised description panel (125 words):** Give workers at chemical plants a protected channel to report substantial-risk findings directly to EPA, with anti-retaliation protection and a duty on the agency to log, investigate, and publish what it receives, within existing trade-secret and occupational-safety law. Congress writes the duty into the chemicals statute; EPA and the OSHA whistleblower program administer it. Any worker can file at any time, and the report triggers a required agency response, so participation is binding on the regulator rather than advisory. The agency publishes its findings. The intervention dates to 1980, when DuPont had confirmed elevated PFOA in its own workers, concluded internally that continued exposure was not tolerable, and began discharging into the Cape Fear, while the workers who saw the harm first had no channel.
9. **Rewritten causal links:**

   - Link 1. *Was:* "A protected channel could produce more disclosures" — A protected, structured worker reporting channel could produce more disclosures of concealed findings.
     *Now:* "A protected channel could produce more disclosures" — A protected, structured reporting channel could produce more disclosures of concealed findings; employees are the largest single detector of corporate fraud, but few report without strong protection.

   - Link 2. *Was:* "Disclosure could reach and move the regulator" — Worker disclosure could reach the regulator and produce scrutiny decades before litigation discovery did.
     *Now:* "Disclosure could reach and move the regulator" — Worker disclosure could reach the regulator and produce scrutiny decades before litigation discovery did; 3M's 1998 disclosure did trigger EPA's first sustained look at PFAS.
