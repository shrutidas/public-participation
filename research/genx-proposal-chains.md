# GenX proposal chains: causal chains, evidence, and counter-evidence

Case: GenX contamination of the Cape Fear River (DuPont/Chemours, Fayetteville Works, NC).
Scope: the six workshop proposals recorded in js/chains/genx-pfas.js, each written out as an
ordered causal chain, with per-link supporting and counter-evidence from web searches run for
this report, comparable real-world interventions with measured outcomes, a mechanisms audit,
and open questions.

Sourcing note. Every quotation below comes from a source fetched with WebFetch during this
research session, except where marked "case file, previously audited," which denotes evidence
and quotes already present in the audited chain or timeline files. Sources that could not be
fetched are marked "unfetched" and carry no quote. Grades: Strong (directly measured, specific,
tightly connected), Moderate (relevant but indirect, partial, or context-limited), Weak
(speculative, analogical, or confounded), UNSTUDIED (no study identified).

Framing constraint from the project record: this was a disclosure failure, not a detection
failure. EPA scientists identified the compounds in samples collected in 2012 (Strynar et al.
2015, case file, previously audited); the public learned in June 2017 from the Wilmington
StarNews. Proposals are evaluated against that 2012 to 2017 window, and against the longer
1961 to 2017 concealment arc where the proposal targets it.

Shared measured endpoints (used across all six chains; all case file, previously audited):

- Serum PFAS in residents: Kotlarz et al. 2020, Environmental Health Perspectives: fluoroethers
  contributed 23% of summed serum PFAS in 344 Wilmington residents (median summed PFAS
  25.0 ng/mL); median fluoroether levels declined 34 to 65% over six months once discharge
  controls were in place. Archived 2010 to 2016 serum: PFMOAA median 42 ng/mL (NC State 2025).
- Cumulative exposure: mean GenX 631 ng/L, up to ~4,500 ng/L in source water (Sun et al. 2016),
  for roughly 200,000 CFPUA customers, over a discharge window running from 1980.
- Discharge reductions: 99% air emission reduction by end-2019, thermal oxidizer at 99.99%
  efficiency (2019 Consent Order).
- Filtration costs: CFPUA granular activated carbon, $43 million built, $82.8 million with
  maintenance, funded by an 8% customer rate increase; Brunswick County reverse osmosis,
  $158.7 million (WUNC 2025; Port City Daily 2022).

Body-burden kinetics, fetched this session: after PFOA exposure ended in Arnsberg, Germany,
"PFOA levels decreased in all study participants from Arnsberg," with a geometric mean
elimination half-life of 3.26 years (Brede et al. 2010, reported in TERA Alliance for Risk
Assessment case summary, https://tera.org/Alliance%20for%20Risk/Workshop%20Xll/CaseSummaryPFOA.pdf).
This supports the terminal link, earlier exposure cutoff to lower body burden, in every chain
below; that link is graded once here as Strong and cross-referenced.

## Proposal 1: Give workers a protected way to report (link D1)

Method: worker committees and protected disclosure. Workshop rationale: DuPont observed
unusual medical issues among its own workers; workers were "the canary in the coal mine"
with no say.

### Causal chain

1. A protected, structured worker reporting channel exists at the fluorochemicals plants.
2. Workers or their representatives report internal medical findings (1961 toxicologist
   warnings; 1970s worker blood PFOA; 1980 "continued exposure is not tolerable" memo;
   1978 to 1981 reassignment of 50 women) to a regulator rather than only to management.
3. The regulator receives TSCA Section 8(e)-type substantial-risk information decades before
   the 2000 litigation discovery and the 2005 EPA penalty.
4. Earlier regulatory scrutiny of PFOA and successor discharges, including the 1980
   Fayetteville Works GenX startup and the 2009 5(e) order.
5. Shorter discharge window; lower cumulative exposure. [MEASURED: source-water
   concentrations, discharge window]
6. Lower serum PFAS body burden in downstream residents. [MEASURED: Kotlarz 2020;
   archived serum; Brede 2010 kinetics]
7. Corporate accountability; worker empowerment. [NON-MEASURED, CONJECTURED: the workshop
   valued these as ends; no measurement exists in this record]

### If only this one thing: when and where

1980, and federally. By 1980 DuPont had confirmed elevated PFOA in Washington Works workers
and concluded internally that "continued exposure is not tolerable" (case file, previously
audited), the same year Fayetteville Works began discharging GenX process chemicals to the
Cape Fear. A protected worker channel with a duty to transmit substantial-risk findings to
EPA belongs in TSCA itself: jurisdiction Congress and EPA, with OSHA anti-retaliation
machinery as the shield. The earliest leverage point is 1961 (first internal warnings), but
1980 is the first year at which the intervention touches this river.

### Comparables with measured outcomes

1. Richard Purdy, 3M, 1999 (case file, previously audited): resignation letter to EPA;
   per the Minnesota Reformer, EPA began investigating the chemicals that year; 3M announced
   the PFOS phaseout in 2000. Individual, unprotected, late; the outcome it moved is documented.
2. OSHA retaliation complaints as deterrent, Wilde 2017 (fetched via ScienceDaily): 317
   firms; measured reductions in misreporting and tax aggressiveness lasting two-plus years.
3. GAO 2010 audit of the OSHA whistleblower program (fetched): the measured baseline of the
   existing channel, documenting internal control failures; a comparable for what the
   proposal must exceed, not what it should copy.

## Proposal 2: Let residents report early warning signs (link D1)

Method: participatory science and community reporting, anchored by the workshop to 1998,
"when someone first notices something is off": local meetings, information spreading,
crowdsourced data collection at the sign of a threat.

### Causal chain

1. A standing institution receives resident reports of suspicions and symptoms and treats
   them as experiential knowledge.
2. Reports trigger investigation (sampling, epidemiology) before formal institutional
   detection exists.
3. Earlier detection of contamination.
4. Earlier regulatory response.
5. Shorter exposure window; lower cumulative exposure. [MEASURED]
6. Lower serum PFAS. [MEASURED, via Kotlarz 2020 and Brede 2010 as above]
7. Experiential knowledge honored; community standing. [NON-MEASURED, CONJECTURED]

### If only this one thing: when and where

The honest reading of the record: on the Cape Fear itself this instrument had little to grip
before 2012, because the contaminant produced no resident-observable signal. Its documented
leverage in the wider PFAS arc is 1998, Wood County, West Virginia, where Wilbur Tennant's
observations existed and the missing piece was an institution that treated them as data
without requiring two decades of litigation; jurisdiction EPA Region 3 and West Virginia.
If the workshop insists on a Cape Fear deployment, the defensible anchor is 2013 to 2015,
when UCMR3 sampling data existed and an organized community sampling program (bucket-brigade
style, water rather than air) could have found and publicized what the utility did not:
jurisdiction NC DEQ and CFPUA.

### Comparables with measured outcomes

1. Woburn, MA (fetched, Kennedy): community-driven study confirmed a fourfold leukemia
   excess; outcomes included an approximately $8 million settlement, a multimillion-dollar
   Superfund cleanup, "a state 'Superfund' to clean up toxic-waste sites," and "a state
   cancer registry."
2. Flint, MI (Citizen Science: Theory and Practice, DOI 10.5334/cstp.154, fetched,
   https://theoryandpractice.citizenscienceassociation.org/articles/10.5334/cstp.154):
   resident-collected sampling in "269 geographically distributed Flint homes"; the paper
   records that "The resulting Federal Emergency declaration in January 2016 resulted in
   more than $600 million in relief funding," and that "all official state water lead data
   before 2016 were collected using so-called 'cheats' that effectively minimized detection
   of lead hazards at the tap."
3. Tonawanda, NY (Swarthmore Global Nonviolent Action Database, fetched,
   https://nvdatabase.swarthmore.edu/content/clean-air-coalition-western-new-york-hold-tonawanda-coke-accountable-air-pollution-2005-2009;
   DOJ press release, fetched, https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/tonawanda-coke-and-manager-sentenced-violating-clean-air-act-and-resource-conservation-and):
   residents "used homemade bucket set-ups to monitor local air quality" and found benzene;
   the state study found "local levels of benzene were 75 times higher than the legal limit";
   Tonawanda Coke was convicted on 14 of 19 charges and sentenced to a "$12.5 million criminal
   penalty" plus "$12.2 million in community service payments" funding an epidemiological
   study and an air and soil study; the environmental control manager received one year in
   prison.

## Proposal 3: Warn the public when discharge is approved (link D2)

Method: statutory right-to-know attached to the approval itself: every discharge approval
carries notification of the affected public and a local monitoring plan. Applied here, the
2009 TSCA 5(e) consent order would have carried a duty to notify downstream water users.

### Causal chain

1. Notification duty plus local monitoring plan attaches to the discharge approval
   (1980 NPDES permit; 2009 5(e) order).
2. Downstream utilities and residents know of the discharge from day one.
3. Disclosure pressure operates: monitoring, avoidance behavior, market and political
   pressure on the firm.
4. The firm reduces discharges preemptively and the regulator verifies; capture failures
   surface in years, not decades.
5. Lower cumulative exposure. [MEASURED endpoints: source-water concentrations, discharge
   window; avoided filtration costs, $82.8M CFPUA plus $158.7M Brunswick]
6. Lower serum PFAS. [MEASURED]
7. Externalities borne knowingly rather than unknowingly; procedural fairness.
   [NON-MEASURED, CONJECTURED]

### If only this one thing: when and where

2009, EPA Office of Chemical Safety and Pollution Prevention. The 5(e) consent order is the
single document where the federal government simultaneously recognized "insufficient
information and potential unreasonable risk" and authorized manufacture (case file, previously
audited); attaching downstream notification and a public local monitoring plan to that order
reaches every affected water user eight years before the newspaper did. The 1980 NPDES permit
at NC DEQ's predecessor is the earlier anchor, but the permit record shows why approval-time
disclosure must be structural rather than applicant-drafted: DEQ found "no evidence in the
permit file indicating that Chemours or DuPont disclosed the discharge to surface water of
GenX compounds" (NC DEQ letter, Sept. 5, 2017 quoted in Nov. 16, 2017 letter, fetched).

### Comparables with measured outcomes

1. Toxics Release Inventory (EPCRA 1986): 21% release decline since 2014 and 32% air decline
   (EPA, fetched); the founding right-to-know disclosure regime.
2. Massachusetts TURA: 66% use, 72% byproduct, 92% on-site release reductions 1990 to 2016
   (TURI, fetched).
3. REACH candidate list: 61% of surveyed German downstream users substituted listed
   substances (Business Chemistry, fetched); a listing-triggered disclosure regime with
   measured substitution behavior.

## Proposal 4: A law that forces public notice on detection (link D3)

Method: a right-to-know trigger: when a peer-reviewed detection above a threshold reaches a
regulator, public notification fires automatically.

### Causal chain

1. Statutory trigger converts regulator receipt of a qualifying detection into public notice.
2. Public knowledge arrives in August 2012 (EPA detection) or November 2016 (Sun et al.
   reaching NC DEQ) instead of June 2017.
3. Agencies respond on the demonstrated timetable: investigation within 7 days, health goal
   within 5 weeks, discharge cessation within weeks (all demonstrated post-June 2017,
   case file, previously audited).
4. Discharge ends roughly 7 months to 5 years earlier than it did.
5. Avoided cumulative exposure across that window at mean 631 ng/L. [MEASURED]
6. Lower serum PFAS; earlier onset of the 34 to 65% six-month declines Kotlarz measured.
   [MEASURED]
7. Journalism removed as single point of failure; institutional trust. [NON-MEASURED,
   CONJECTURED]

The in-case record shows the trigger's absence was the entire gap: the UCMR3 data were public, and "he loaded the data to see what would pop up for the Wilmington area. There were some local hits" (Coastal Review 2022 on Hagerty, case file, previously audited); one reporter performed the function once. | The Public Notification Rule "does not address unregulated contaminants" (EPA, fetched); no jurisdiction was identified with a detection-triggered notice law for unregulated contaminants (consistent with chain file gap note). | Strong as within-case counterfactual logic; UNSTUDIED as enacted policy. |

### If only this one thing: when and where

August 2012, EPA. The highest-leverage single placement is a federal trigger firing on EPA's
own research detection (Strynar samples collected summer 2012), jurisdiction Congress writing
the duty into SDWA or TSCA and EPA executing it; that converts a five-year silence into weeks.
The state-scale fallback is November 2016, NC DEQ, on receipt of the Sun et al. paper, which
would still have cut roughly seven months of exposure and, more importantly, would have made
the disclosure institutional rather than journalistic. This proposal has the tightest fit of
the six to the project's central finding, because it targets the exact link that failed.

### Comparables with measured outcomes

1. SDWA Public Notification Rule (EPA, fetched): the enacted template, 24-hour Tier 1 notice,
   measured only in the sense of being operational nationwide; its regulated-only scope is
   the boundary this proposal erases.
2. Flint (fetched): once resident sampling made contamination public, emergency declarations
   and $600 million followed; the case measures what public knowledge converts into.
3. The in-case comparator: Hagerty's June 2017 story followed by a 7-day state response
   (case file, previously audited); the proposal is that story, made statutory.

## Proposal 5: Let the public choose experts to review the evidence (link R1)

Method: independent science review with community-appointed experts, third-party assessment
of blood panels and water analyses, and a defined public role; the workshop named the
precondition as trust, or a bypass around untrusted authorities.

### Causal chain

1. The community appoints experts to an independent review panel at emergency onset.
2. The panel independently verifies testing (blood, water) and translates findings.
3. Risk communication runs through a trusted channel; the 71,000-to-140 ppt health-goal
   whiplash of June to July 2017 arrives explained rather than as institutional caprice.
4. Sustained community engagement and study participation; calibrated protective behavior.
5. Exposure and remediation outcomes as in other chains. [MEASURED endpoints exist in the
   case, but no link from panel composition to those endpoints is measured anywhere]
6. Legitimacy of the response; repaired trust. [NON-MEASURED, CONJECTURED: this is the
   workshop's actual target]

## Proposal 6: Federal funding, enforcement designed locally (link R6)

Method: federally funded programs whose enforcement and implementation are locally designed;
the workshop's route from facility-level wins to protections that reach the next community.

### Causal chain

1. Federal funds arrive paired with locally designed enforcement and implementation.
2. Local institutions gain standing capacity (monitoring, legal, technical) beyond one
   facility fight.
3. Facility-level wins convert into generalizable standards (state MCLs, surface-water
   standards).
4. The next community is protected before exposure, not after. [MEASURED endpoint would be
   statewide discharge and exposure reductions; in this case the endpoint is measured in the
   negative: no enforceable NC surface-water standard nine years on, federal MCL proposed
   for rescission May 2026 (case file, previously audited)]
5. Durable accountability independent of any single order. [NON-MEASURED, CONJECTURED]

February 2019, at Consent Order entry; jurisdictions Congress and EPA for the funding

### Efficacy of the participation that did occur

- Cape Fear River Watch and SELC litigation: the highest documented efficacy in the case.

  reductions (case file, previously audited). No formal evaluation of the party-status model
  exists (chain gap note); the outcome documentation is nonetheless itemized and primary.
- Consent Order public comment: effective by the agency's own response-to-comments document;
  agency-acknowledged added protections for non-party downstream communities (case file,
  previously audited).

  growing cohort (344 to 1,020), and runs an eight-year return-of-results practice. Its
