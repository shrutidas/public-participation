# Source audit handoff

Written 2026-09-03, at the end of the round that found a fabricated citation. This is the
brief for a fresh chat that audits every source in the tool. Read it first, then read
`research/source-audit-worklist.csv`.

## Why this exists

The "Local school boards and public comment" mechanism in `js/spine/covid-schools.js` cited
`https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-revolt-of-the-public-school-parents/`. That URL
returns 404. No Brookings article by that title or slug exists. A title search and a slug
search both come back empty. The citation was invented in an early round, not fetched.

The two claims it was supposed to back are real. Both already carried live sources in
`js/chains/covid-schools.js`, so the mechanism now cites those:

- 6% to 49% of students in high-participation districts: Brookings school board survey,
  Chapter 2 (Valant, Bertrand, Perera, Zerbino, June 5 2026). Fetched and verified. The
  quote in the data is verbatim.
- 21 of the 25 most conflictual months since 2015 came after January 2020: Holman, Johnson
  and Simko, `https://edworkingpapers.com/ai24-1102`.

Fixed in commit `d6d8525`. That commit is not pushed yet.

The point of the audit is that one fabricated citation means the population may hold more.
Nothing about the fix tells us how many.

## What lint checks, and what it misses

`scripts/lint-cases.mjs` checks that a source has a non-empty URL and that the URL starts
with `http://` or `https://`. That is all. It does not resolve the URL, does not check the
page exists, and does not compare the quote to the page. Lint passed with the dead link in
place for every round it sat there.

So a clean lint run says nothing about whether a source is real.

## The inventory

353 unique URLs across 558 citations. They live in three layers, all under `js/`:

- `js/cases/` (covid-schools, covid-vaccines, genx-pfas, meta-youth): timeline entries
- `js/chains/` (three cases): causal chain links, evidence and counter-evidence cards
- `js/spine/` (three cases): mechanisms, measured impacts, proposals, comparable cases

174 of the 353 URLs carry at least one verbatim quote in the data. Those are the cheapest to
falsify, because the quote either appears on the page or it does not.

Rough risk tiers, by URL shape:

| Tier | Count | What it is | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 89 | Journal, DOI, PMC, PubMed, NBER, RePEc, EdWorkingPapers | Low. A DOI or a PMC ID either resolves or it does not, and the identifier is hard to invent convincingly. |
| B | 93 | .gov and .edu | Medium. Real agencies, but agencies reorganize URLs and PDFs move. |
| C | 171 | Press, think tanks, advocacy orgs, everything else | High. This is the shape the fabricated one had: a plausible outlet plus a plausible slug. |

104 of the C-tier URLs carry no verbatim quote at all. Those are the highest risk in the
whole set. Nothing anchors them to a real page.

Start there.

## The worklist

`research/source-audit-worklist.csv`, one row per unique URL, generated by
`python3 scripts/source-worklist.py > research/source-audit-worklist.csv`. Columns:

- `URL`, `Domain`
- `Labels Used`: every `source()` label the repo attaches to this URL, separated by ` | `
- `Citations`: how many cards cite it
- `Cases`, `Record Types`
- `Verbatim Quotes`: how many of those citations carry a quote
- `Deep Links`: the in-tool routes to the cards, so you can open each one
- `HTTP Status`, `Page Exists`, `Claim Supported`, `Verdict`, `Notes`: blank, for you to fill

The claim text lives in `research-repository.csv`, one row per citation, columns `Key
Finding`, `Verbatim Quote`, `Caveat`. Join the two on `URL`.

Do not hand-edit `research-repository.csv`. It is generated. Fix the source data under `js/`
and re-run `node scripts/export-research.mjs > research-repository.csv`.

## Method

Per URL:

1. Fetch it. Record the status.
2. If it 404s or redirects to a homepage or a search page, treat the page as gone. Search
   for the title and for the slug before calling it fabricated. A moved page and an invented
   page look identical at the HTTP layer and are different problems.
3. If it resolves, check the page is what the label says it is. Right author, right
   publisher, right year. A real page under a wrong label is its own error.
4. For every citation of that URL, check the claim. If the data carries a verbatim quote,
   confirm the quote appears on the page, word for word. If there is no quote, confirm the
   page states the finding.
5. Record a verdict.

## Verdict vocabulary

Use exactly these, so the results sort:

- `VERIFIED`: page exists, is what the label says, and supports the claim.
- `QUOTE MISMATCH`: page exists and supports the claim, but the verbatim quote is not
  verbatim.
- `MOVED`: the content exists at a different URL. Put the new URL in `Notes`.
- `WRONG LABEL`: page exists, claim holds, label names the wrong author, publisher or year.
- `CLAIM UNSUPPORTED`: page exists but does not say what the card says it says.
- `PAYWALLED`: cannot verify from the public page. Note whether the abstract supports the
  claim.
- `FABRICATED`: no such page, and a title and slug search finds no such work anywhere.

`FABRICATED` and `CLAIM UNSUPPORTED` are the findings that matter. Surface those first and
separately. Do not bury them in a table of 353 rows.

## A second thing to check while you are in there

## Constraints you will hit

- **No shell can fetch these URLs.** The device VM has no network at all. The cloud
  container's egress proxy refuses CONNECT to arbitrary hosts, so curl returns `000`. Only
  WebFetch and WebSearch reach the open web. A 353-URL sweep therefore needs fan-out
  subagents, not a shell loop. Budget for that.
- **Committing works in the VM, pushing does not.** Commit with
  `git -c user.name='Shruti Das' -c user.email='shrutid647@gmail.com' commit`. Push fails
  with "could not read Username for https://github.com". Pushing from the cloud container
  fails too: the proxy will not inject a credential for this repo. She pushes from her own
  terminal.
- **The VM strands git lock files.** The mount blocks unlink, so `.git/index.lock` and
  `.git/HEAD.lock` survive every git call and block the next one. `mv` is allowed. Move them
  into `_to_delete/git-locks/` after the last git command.
- **`export-research.mjs` writes the CSV to stdout** and its summary to stderr. Redirect
  stdout to the file.
