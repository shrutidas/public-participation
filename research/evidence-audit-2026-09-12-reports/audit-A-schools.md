# Audit A: COVID schools, Proposal 1 (stakeholder council), claims "local fit" and "chronic absence"

File audited: js/spine/covid-schools.js (read only, nothing edited).

Proposal: "A council of parents, teachers, and experts designs the reopening plan" (Stakeholder Advisory Council).

Implication sentence (outcome text): "People who know the buildings design reopening plans that fit each school, which builds trust between parents and teachers, keeps teachers, and cuts chronic absenteeism."

Lens used: does handing design to people close to the school produce decisions that match local conditions and priorities (the reviewers' "locally sensitive trade-offs"), under what conditions, and does that replicate. Where a synthesis exists it replaces the single studies it contains.

## Claim cl/2: "Plans designed close to the school fit its local constraints."

Card count now: 4 evidence, 4 counter, 1 case. After audit: 3 evidence, 3 counter, 0 case.

The key fit test. The outcome the claim names is fit: decisions that track local need, conditions, or priorities. Test scores, teacher hiring, and capture are neighboring outcomes. Only Faguet (need-tracking), the two US reopening papers (decisions vs local virus severity), and partly Schueler measure the claim's outcome.

Cases: none (the delegation card is circular).

Honest balance. Yes, with one warning the map should state plainly. The quality-weighted syntheses find that local control modestly improves how well services match local needs, and a large experiment shows local deciders apply their own idea of need and leave people more satisfied. The US evidence from 2020 itself runs the other way on the open or closed choice: districts tracked politics rather than the virus. It is less political on operations. The scope conditions (meeting formats hand power to elites; local control helps least where parents have low standing next to staff) bear directly on large urban districts. No study tests the proposal's exact mechanism, a stakeholder council designing school operations in the US, so the claim rests on analogy. Removing Duflo and Pradhan removes two strong positive experiments, but neither measured fit, and the Campbell review that contains them enters the set.

- US Fall 2020 Delegation to School Districts: CIRCULAR. It is the case's own timeline event (48 states left the fall choice to districts), and it duplicates the Grossmann et al. card. Put the delegation fact on the timeline and let the merged Hartney and Finger card carry the finding.

Considered and not added: Hanushek, Link and Woessmann 2013, Journal of Development Economics (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2012.08.002). It is the best high-income evidence that school autonomy transports, and it tempers ADD-3 for the US. Not added because its outcome is achievement, not fit, and Channa and Faguet already include it. Worth a sentence in ADD-3's caveat if the group wants the transport point shown.

Card count now: 4 evidence, 2 counter, 0 case. After audit: 3 evidence, 2 counter.

The key fit test. "Work closely" means two-way, relational contact (meetings, home visits, partnership programs). One-way mailings and texts test a neighboring claim about informing parents.

Honest balance. Yes. The kept set shows three causal or quasi-causal positive findings, two of them in US districts after or near the pandemic, against a meta-analysis in which family-school partnership programs were the one category whose effect could be zero, and a Chicago experiment where relational outreach did nothing in the early grades. That matches the literature: promising, small, and conditional on age and on doing the contact one-on-one. Cutting the two messaging cards together removes one strong positive and one weak positive, so the balance does not shift. Two cautions for the map. The NYC effect comes from a bundle of services, not family work alone. And the proposal's link to absence is indirect: a reopening council is not itself a family engagement program, so these cards show that close school-family work lowers absence, not that a design council does.

### New cards for cl/5

- Type: evidence (ADD-4, replaces Cook et al. 2017)
  Headline: When Connecticut school staff visited the homes of chronically absent students after the pandemic, those students came to school more.
  Finding: In 2021 and 2022, fifteen Connecticut districts sent teachers, counselors, and community partners to the homes of 8,690 students who had been chronically absent. The evaluators compared students visited earlier with students visited later. Attendance rose about four percentage points in the month after the first visit and kept climbing in later months. One district saw no gain: "In contrast, LEAP appears to have had no impact on attendance rates in New Haven Public Schools." That district had canvassed neighborhoods instead of making one-on-one visits. The study is a state-commissioned evaluation by university researchers, not a journal article.
  Quote: "for the full sample of students treated by LEAP, other than those from New Haven Public Schools, attendance rates increased by approximately four percentage points in the month immediately following the first LEAP visit."
  Quote fetched from: https://portal.ct.gov/ccerc/-/media/CCERC/Reports/CCERC-Report-LEAP_FINAL.pdf
  Source label: Stemler, Werblow, Brunner and colleagues 2022, Center for Connecticut Education Research Collaboration
  URL: https://portal.ct.gov/ccerc/-/media/CCERC/Reports/CCERC-Report-LEAP_FINAL.pdf

  Grade: moderate
  Verified: YES (quote found in the fetched PDF text; the New Haven sentence is from the same page)

- Type: evidence (ADD-5, replaces Sheldon 2007)
  Headline: When New York City turned struggling schools into community schools with family and social services, chronic absence fell right away and stayed down.
  Finding: In 2015 New York City made its lowest-performing schools community schools, adding health and social services, longer days, and family education. Comparing schools just above and below the cutoffs used to select them, the authors report: "We find an immediate reduction in chronic absenteeism of 5.6 percentage points, which persists over the following three years." Math and reading scores rose later. The program bundles many supports, so the study cannot separate the family work from the rest.
  Quote: "We find an immediate reduction in chronic absenteeism of 5.6 percentage points, which persists over the following three years."

## Searches run and what they found

1. Campbell systematic review on school-based decision-making: found Carr-Hill, Rolleston, Schendel 2016 (Campbell) and the 2018 journal version; the pre-publication PDF confirms Duflo et al. and Pradhan et al. are included and Galiani et al. is not.
2. Channa and Faguet 2016 decentralization review: found the abstract (preference matching in education) and the World Bank open PDF; confirmed it includes Faguet 2004, Galiani 2008, and Hanushek et al.
3. Meta-analysis of chronic absenteeism interventions: found Eklund et al. 2020 (family-school partnership effect could be zero) and Li et al. 2026 (small overall effect, no family breakout in the abstract). Pulled Eklund's reference list from OpenAlex; Sheldon 2007 is in it, Cook 2017 is not.
4. NYC community schools causal evaluation: found the RAND report and the peer-reviewed Covelli, Engberg, Opper 2025 regression discontinuity study in JREE.
5. Randomized or causal studies of teacher home visits and chronic absence: no US randomized trial found. Found Connecticut's LEAP evaluation (staggered-timing comparison, 15 districts, 2021 and 2022) and a correlational Parent Teacher Home Visits report (Sheldon and Jung 2018, not used).
6. Peer-reviewed version of the LEAP study: none found; the state-commissioned report is the primary source.
7. Communities In Schools randomized trial: MDRC 2017 found no attendance effect from student case management; judged off-fit.
8. Hanushek, Link, Woessmann 2013 school autonomy panel: confirmed abstract on RePEc; autonomy helps in developed systems, hurts in low-performing ones. Held in reserve.
9. Alatas et al. 2012 community targeting experiment: abstract confirmed via Europe PMC.
10. Abstracts for all existing cards on both claims re-fetched from OpenAlex, Crossref, or Europe PMC; every DOI returned a 302 redirect from doi.org.
