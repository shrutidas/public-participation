# Writing and Research Audit, August 27, 2026

Scope: the three spine cases (schools, vaccines, GenX), their 16 proposals and 57 chain links, 14 measured impacts, and 51 comparable cases. This audit covers what the research actually supports, where the writing read as machine-generated, and what changed today. Everything below is applied in the data files unless marked "flagged, not changed."

## 1. The research we actually have

The repository now carries 617 rows and 351 unique sources (up from 583 and 323). The evidence base divides into three tiers.

Tier 1, measured in the case itself: mode-effect studies for schools (Dee, Goldhaber, Jack), the mandate uptake record (Nature Human Behaviour, Rains, now Ward 2022), the Cape Fear exposure and blood record (Sun 2016, Kotlarz 2020), and the post-2017 enforcement outcomes. This tier is solid and fetched, with verbatim quotes.

Tier 2, comparable cases from other countries and domains: all 23 cases in your workshop-notes digest are present in the tool's data, including GM Nation, Oregon CIR, Ostbelgien, the French climate convention, Korea's Shin-Gori deliberation, Calgary fluoridation, Uganda clinic monitoring, the Finnish and Swedish nuclear siting veto, Flint, Tonawanda, and the C8 panel. The problem was visibility, not coverage: they rendered only in the sidebar after clicking a proposal. They now also appear as a named strip on the map under each chain and at the bottom of every link detail.

Tier 3, the 14 links graded unstudied. Deep research this round found reputable adjacent evidence for 12 of them (see section 4). Two remain genuinely bare: the Cape Fear-specific resident-detection claim (counter-evidence dominates: GenX gave residents nothing to observe) and better-allocation-raises-uptake (untestable by construction; Rwanda pay-for-performance added as the nearest anchor).

Known remaining gaps: Konar and Cohen 1997 (TRI disclosure and firm behavior) still to add when you obtain it; no human disease study of GenX itself exists; the mortality cost of the spring 2021 vaccination lag has never been estimated by race; no US pre-post measurement of regulator trust after a disclosure failure.

## 2. GenX measured impacts, audited one by one

Exposure through drinking water: sound. Kept.

PFAS measured in the blood of residents: sound but it stopped short of the point. Blood is a marker; the harm is disease. The measures text now says why blood matters and points to the new disease card. "Serum" replaced with "blood" throughout the case.

Disease linked to PFAS exposure: NEW, the sixth impact. What it carries: Rosen et al. 2022 (EHP) measured higher total and non-HDL cholesterol with PFOS and PFNA in the Wilmington cohort itself, about 5 to 6 mg/dL per quartile, strongest in older adults. The C8 Science Panel's probable links (kidney cancer, testicular cancer, thyroid disease, ulcerative colitis, high cholesterol, pregnancy-induced hypertension) from about 69,000 PFOA-exposed residents. IARC's 2023 classification of PFOA as carcinogenic to humans, with limited human evidence for renal cell and testicular cancer. The 2024 study report-back: 97 percent of participants above 2 ng/mL summed PFAS, 12 percent above the 20 ng/mL medical-monitoring threshold. Counter-evidence card states plainly that no human disease study of GenX itself exists and EPA's assessment rests on animal studies. The arrow from the discharge is graded moderate for that reason. Chains that reach blood now also reach disease, and their final links say why blood matters.

Filtration costs paid by water customers: kept; cut the editorial coda "the costs are the measured price of decades of unknowing exposure."

Discharge cut 99 percent, a $12 million penalty: kept; "serum fluoroethers" now "fluoroether levels in residents' blood."

Statewide rule adoption: sound. Kept.

## 3. The writing audit

What read as machine-generated, and the pattern behind it:

Declarative claims for hypothetical chains. Nearly every chain link asserted its step as fact ("A protected channel produces disclosures") when the whole point of a proposal chain is that it did not happen. Every hypothetical link now reads as a proposal: "A protected, structured worker reporting channel could produce more disclosures of concealed findings." Links stating documented general facts keep the declarative ("Higher uptake prevents deaths," "Ventilation and outdoor options make school safer," "Access raises uptake"), because the evidence is real regardless of the proposal. About 30 link names and claims changed across the three cases.

Jargon. "Body burden" survived in three link names and claims despite the earlier de-jargoning pass; all gone. "Serum" replaced with "blood" in card text. "Modality" replaced with plain phrasing ("how school runs," "the decision"). "The open-closed binary" is now "open against closed."

Analyst-voice tics, replaced with plain statements: "the canary in the coal mine with no say," "the response function is demonstrated fast when it fires," "approval-time naming is the load-bearing step," "the measured quantities on both sides of the counterfactual," "that absence is itself the finding," "the proposal is this story, made statutory" (which also appeared twice in one proposal), "notice going out against the public coming in," and the sidebar's "That absence is a finding, and it is what the flag records" (now "No study tests this link directly").

Repetition. "34 to 65 percent within six months" appeared five times in the GenX file. It survives where it is the evidence for a specific link but no longer pads impact cards it does not belong to.

## 4. New evidence added to formerly bare links

Schools: binding remit (Olken's Indonesia experiment on binding against advisory formats; Gonçalves on participatory budgeting and infant mortality); legitimacy holds enrollment (Tyler and Jackson; Verboon and Goslinga on fair process); safer options change the decision (Hjort et al., mayors adopt policies at +10 points when informed; CDC test-to-stay); local decisions track conditions (Faguet on Bolivia; Fishkin's America in One Room); shared understanding holds confidence (Cucciniello 187-study review; Grimmelikhuijsen experiment, honestly mixed); maintained trust reduces exit (Alsan and Wanamaker on Tuskegee and care avoidance; Musaddiq on pandemic exit); upstream voice reduces lawsuits (Kachalia, University of Michigan disclosure program, 61 percent legal-cost drop).

Vaccines: legitimacy improves compliance (Murphy's Australian lockdown survey; Walters and Bolger meta-analysis); evidence changes decisions (Hjort; DellaVigna, cities adopt tested nudges 27 percent of the time); better allocation (Basinga, Rwanda). Plus your Ward et al. 2022 Nature Medicine paper on the French health pass, entered on the mandates link as both evidence (coverage 49 to 89 percent) and counter-evidence (doubts among the vaccinated rose 44 to 61 percent; the elderly and marginalized lagged), and folded into the France 2016-2018 comparable.

GenX: resident detection (Roy and Edwards on Flint's 269-home dataset; Seto's community sensor network finding 51 percent of episodes officials missed); trusted channels (Frisbee, C8 Health Project's 69,030 enrollment; Boronow and Rochester on exposure report-back changing behavior); public constituency gets plans verified (Kenney's evaluation of 13 Good Neighbor Agreements; Colmer's 130,000 Texas complaints).

## 5. Grade changes, flagged for your review

Where new evidence bears directly on the mechanism, the link moved from unstudied to weak; one moved to moderate. These are judgment calls and sit inside the Bailey A1-A4 question about what chains may claim:

Moved to weak: a binding remit could give non-union voices force (schools); safer options could change the decision (schools); maintained trust could reduce exit and absence (schools); upstream voice could reduce lawsuits and recalls (schools); legitimacy could improve compliance (vaccines); trusted channels could sustain engagement (GenX); a public constituency could get the plan verified (GenX).

Moved to moderate: evidence could change later decisions (vaccines), on the strength of two direct experiments (Hjort; DellaVigna).

Still unstudied, now with adjacent evidence attached: legitimacy could hold enrollment; experts with the public could widen options; local decisions could track conditions; shared understanding could hold confidence; reports could produce detection on the Cape Fear; better allocation could raise uptake.

Also flagged, not changed: "Less exposure could mean less disease" (GenX worker chain, formerly "Shorter window, lower body burden") keeps its strong grade from the blood-level evidence, but the claim now extends to disease, where the support is moderate. If you or Bailey prefer, the grade could drop to moderate to match the claim's reach.
