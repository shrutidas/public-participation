Example: one proposal in the implication, outcome, claims structure

Citizens' assembly proposal from the schools case, using the evidence already in the tool. Read left to right: the proposal predicts something (the arrow), each proposal has one green outcome, tied to a Measured Impact on the left of the map, and the claims and evidence that support the prediction sit under the arrow as a flat list, not a chain.

Proposed public participation
Let randomly selected residents deliberate and decide how schools reopen
Mechanism

Citizens' assembly with decision authority

The assembly can feed a decision in several ways: a binding decision, a referendum, or a vote by the elected school board that must answer the assembly's recommendation.
Proposed implication
Shared understanding and decision-making holds confidence in public schools
ClaimAn assembly can be convened in time to inform the decision.
  • EvidenceA UK citizen panel deliberated online on COVID exit rules within weeks, during lockdownAda Lovelace Institute, 2020
  • CounterLarge citizens' assemblies in Ireland and France took six to nine monthsOECD, 2020
  • Case studyMichigan Sortition Panel, 11/2020Carnegie Mellon, 2021
ClaimA binding decision rule gives residents real force.
  • EvidenceWhen villagers voted directly on local projects, they trusted the result more, even though the projects barely changedOlken, 2008
  • EvidenceBrazilian cities that let residents decide part of the budget spent more on health and saw fewer infant deathsGonçalves, 2014
  • CounterFrance promised to adopt nearly all of its citizen assembly's proposals, then adopted about 40%KNOCA, 2022
  • Case studyFrench Citizens Convention for Climate, 2019-2021KNOCA, 2022
ClaimAn assembly process raises the perceived legitimacy of the decision.
  • EvidencePeople see a decision as fairer when a citizen panel recommended it and officials followed the adviceGermann, Marien & Muradova, 2024
  • EvidenceWatching a school board meeting run as a real discussion raised trust in local officialsCollins, 2021
  • CounterThe fairness gain disappeared when officials ignored the panel's adviceGermann et al., 2024
  • Case studyIrish Citizens' Assembly on the Eighth Amendment, 2016-2018Suiter et al., 2022
ClaimRandom selection surfaces views that open meetings and surveys miss.
  • EvidenceRandomly recruited groups reached different conclusions than people who showed up on their ownOpen University, 2003
  • EvidenceAfter 2020, school board public comment was dominated by the angriest voicesHolman, Johnson & Simko, 2024
  • Case studyOregon Citizens' Initiative Review, 2010 to presentKnobloch, Barthel & Gastil, 2020
What changed from the current build. One green outcome per proposal, written as a specific sentence that names the same thing as a Measured Impact in the first column. The tie stays in the data (for highlighting the matching impact card on select) but is not spelled out in words. Everything the proposal is argued to do sits under it as claims, and every claim carries evidence. Claims are a flat list under each arrow, with the evidence stated in one line rather than a bare citation. Case studies sit under the claim they support, flagged CASE. A green node is filled only when it matches a Measured Impact on the left of the map. The two empty implications in the data ("Depolarized decision", "Cross-regional learning") are dropped until they have a claim. The "effects it aims at" field is gone from the card; the note about integration paths absorbs the dropped school-board proposal.
