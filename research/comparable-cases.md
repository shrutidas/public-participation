# Comparable Real-World Cases for the Project's Participation Proposals

Cross-domain sweep, August 2026. Every quoted passage comes verbatim from a source fetched during this sweep; sources that could not be fetched are marked "unfetched" and carry no quotes. Evidence strength: Strong = directly measured, specific outcome tied to the intervention; Moderate = relevant but indirect, partial, or context-limited; Weak = speculative, analogical, or confounded.

A recurring pattern across families: process quality and reach are measured often; downstream outcomes (trust, compliance, health) are measured rarely. The families with the best-measured outcomes in this sweep are community monitoring of services (Uganda), citizen environmental sampling (Tonawanda, Flint), and statutory disclosure (drinking water reports), none of which is a deliberative mini-public.

---

## 1. Citizens assemblies and mini-publics with real authority

Maps to: schools chain S1 ("Regional citizen assemblies that can decide") and S5; vaccines chain V5 ("Public deliberation before any mandate").

### 1a. Irish Citizens' Assembly and the 2018 abortion referendum (Ireland, 2016-2018)

What was tried. A government-sponsored assembly of 99 randomly selected citizens plus an independent chair met over five weekends from November 2016 to April 2017 on the constitutional status of abortion, with expert presentations, advocacy presentations, and facilitated small-group deliberation. Formally advisory, but coupled by design to a parliamentary committee and a national referendum, which gives its output a binding path that most mini-publics lack.

Measured outcomes. The assembly voted for substantial liberalization; the May 25, 2018 referendum repealed the Eighth Amendment with 66% in favor on 64% turnout, a result the fetched analysis describes as notably similar to the assembly's own vote. Government adopted the assembly's suggested constitutional wording over the parliamentary committee's. Suiter, Farrell, Harris, and Murphy (Political Studies Review, 2022, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/14789299211020909) measured deliberative quality with linguistic analysis of 3,981 speech acts across both forums and found "Members of the Citizens' Assembly demonstrate a deeper cognitively complex grasp of the subject matter" than parliamentarians, and that experts displayed higher cognitive complexity when addressing citizens than when addressing the parliamentary committee.

Caution. The referendum result is a policy-adoption outcome, not a causal estimate of the assembly's contribution; no counterfactual exists for what the referendum would have returned without the assembly.

Chain mapping. Schools S1 (the proposal's model case for sortition plus a binding path); vaccines V5 (deliberation on a value tradeoff before a coercive constitutional change).

Evidence strength. Strong for policy adoption and for measured deliberative quality; Moderate for any causal claim about the assembly changing the outcome.

### 1b. French Citizens' Convention for Climate (France, 2019-2021)

What was tried. 150 randomly selected citizens deliberated on national climate policy with an explicit presidential promise to pass their proposals "without filter." The promise was not kept in full, which makes this the best-documented case of a quasi-binding commitment eroding.

Measured outcomes. The KNOCA briefing by Averchenkova, Koehl, and Smith (Grantham Research Institute / KNOCA, 2022, https://www.knoca.eu/briefings/policy-impact-of-the-french-citizens-convention-for-the-climate-untangling-the-fate-of-the-citizens-recommendations) tracked all 149 recommendations: "Twenty percent of recommendations have been implemented, with another 51 percent implemented in a modified form." The analysis also found "the government has been selective in prioritising recommendations based on incentives rather than regulation" and was "more responsive to those recommendations that were more detailed."

Chain mapping. Schools S1 and vaccines V5, as the cautionary complement to Ireland: what happens to a binding promise under political pressure. Roughly 29% of recommendations were dropped, and modification rather than adoption was the modal fate.

Evidence strength. Strong for the implementation count (a directly measured policy-adoption outcome); the count does not measure legitimacy or trust effects.

### 1c. South Korea, Shin-Gori 5 and 6 reactor deliberation (2017)

What was tried. The Moon administration suspended construction of two partly built nuclear reactors and convened a national deliberative process it committed in advance to accept. Per Participedia (https://participedia.net/case/6875), 471 citizens were selected by stratified random sampling from an initial survey pool of 20,000 across "region (from 16 metropolitan cities and provinces), gender, and age group, totalling 160 distinct strata," and deliberated from "September 13, 2017 until October 15, 2017."

Measured outcomes. "59.5% of 471 respondents supported resuming construction on Shin-Kori 5 and 6," while "53.2% of respondents advocated reducing the use of nuclear power in the long run." "President Moon accepted the verdict" and "announced the resumption of construction on Shin-Kori 5 and 6 on October 22, 2017" (Participedia; see also Stanford Deliberative Democracy Lab, https://deliberation.stanford.edu/news/deliberation-south-korea-closing-two-nuclear-reactors, reporting "59.5% Support Resuming Construction while 40.5% Support Permanent Suspension"). The government reversed its own preferred policy on the strength of the result, a rare instance of a pre-commitment honored against the convener's position.

Caution. Wave-by-wave opinion shift percentages were not in the fetched pages; academic analyses of the opinion dynamics (Applied Energy 2020; Energy Policy 2020) went unfetched.

Chain mapping. Vaccines V5 directly: a contested, technically loaded, coercion-adjacent national decision routed through deliberation before the decision, with an honored pre-commitment. Also schools S1.

Evidence strength. Strong for policy adoption tied to the process; Moderate for opinion-change internals (unfetched).

## 2. Mini-publics pairing experts with the public

Maps to: schools chain S1 ("Bring the right experts together with the public").

### 2a. Irish Citizens' Assembly expert engagement (see 1a)

The Suiter et al. (2022) finding doubles as the best measured result in this family: the same experts communicated with more cognitive complexity to a citizen assembly than to a parliamentary committee, a measured interaction effect between forum design and expert behavior. Evidence strength: Strong for the measured linguistic outcome; the outcome is a process measure, not a downstream one.

### 2b. GM Nation? public debate (United Kingdom, 2003)

What was tried. A government-commissioned national debate on GM crop commercialization: six regional events cascading into several hundred open meetings, roughly 37,000 feedback forms, plus ten "narrow-but-deep" recruited groups meant to correct self-selection (Open University summary, https://www.open.edu/openlearn/science-maths-technology/achieving-public-dialogue/content-section-4). Advisory only.

Measured and null outcomes. The final report concluded "people are generally uneasy about GM" and "there was little support for early commercialization." The fetched evaluation summary records two negative findings of direct use to this project: the open meetings attracted self-selecting participants with strong prior opinions, and the debate "lacked clear connection to actual government policy decisions," which "fostered cynicism about the exercise's genuine purpose." The recruited narrow-but-deep groups reached measurably different positions from the self-selected meetings (fewer fixed positions, more uncertainty, delay rather than abandonment), which is itself a measured demonstration that open-door formats and sortition formats produce different outputs from the same population. The Horlick-Jones/Pidgeon evaluation (Science as Culture, 2007, https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14636770601032858) is unfetched (403).

Chain mapping. Schools S1 and S5: a large consultative process without a binding path, producing cynicism rather than legitimacy; the closest documented analog to the chains' "surveys and town halls" failure mode at national scale.

Evidence strength. Moderate; the self-selection contrast is measured, the cynicism finding is qualitative.

### 2c. Danish consensus conferences (Denmark, 1987 onward)

What was tried. Lay panels questioning expert witnesses on technology policy (gene technology, food irradiation, and others), run by the Danish Board of Technology for parliament. Advisory.

Measured outcomes. The one impact study located (Joss, Science and Public Policy, 1998, https://academic.oup.com/spp/article/25/1/2/1688163) surveyed and interviewed members of parliament and polled public awareness; the accessible text reports the conceptual conclusion that "participation should be understood as a facilitating mechanism of rather than a substitute for, technology assessment by the representative decision-making institutions" but the quantitative results sit behind a paywall (unfetched). No measured policy, trust, or behavioral outcome for the canonical consensus conference model was obtained in this sweep, and none of the fetched material claims one exists.

Evidence strength. Weak. The most-cited model in this family appears to run on reputation rather than measured outcomes.

## 3. Devolved and confederal decision-making in a crisis

Maps to: schools chain S2 ("Let different places decide for themselves").

### 3a. Cross-national decentralization and COVID-19 containment timing (93 countries, 2020)

What was tried and measured. Not an intervention but the natural experiment the proposal implies. A Publius: The Journal of Federalism study (2023, https://academic.oup.com/publius/article/54/1/34/7191229) combined Johns Hopkins case data with ACAPS policy tracking across 93 countries and the Hooghe et al. regional authority indices, and found "more decentralized countries delayed the start of containment measures," with the delay concentrated in business closures and domestic travel restrictions; shared-rule arrangements showed no significant relationship.

Chain mapping. Schools S2, as counter-evidence: the proposal argues decentralized decisions track local conditions better; the best cross-national measurement available here finds decentralization bought delay in the containment phase. This does not test the proposal's actual claim (that decentralized deliberation matches decisions to local conditions in the reopening phase), but it is the nearest measured neighbor and it points the other way for the emergency phase. The Swiss fragmented-crisis-management literature (Mavrot, European Policy Analysis, 2024) went unfetched.

Evidence strength. Moderate for the schools chain (different phase, different outcome variable), Strong internally.

### 3b. Municipal veto in Finnish and Swedish nuclear waste siting (1980s-2020s)

What was tried. Both countries gave host municipalities formal veto power over spent fuel repository siting, plus funded independent municipal review. Per Bellona's account (2016, https://bellona.org/news/nuclear-issues/radioactive-waste-and-spent-nuclear-fuel/2016-08-21710), Sweden financed municipal expertise through the Nuclear Waste Fund: Östhammar's repository staff "paid by the Swedish Nuclear Waste Fund, enabling independent assessment rather than industry reliance," and SKB reported "strong local support for a future spent fuel repository" in Östhammar. Finland's Eurajoki representatives, asked about exercising the veto, answered "the process that there is is satisfactory to us." Finland licensed and built Onkalo, the world's first operating deep repository; the fetched page predates final licensing.

Measured outcomes. Local acceptance is reported in surveys the fetched source describes but does not quantify; the striking outcome is behavioral (municipalities with a veto consented, and construction proceeded, where non-consensual programs like Yucca Mountain stalled). The fetched source flags the obvious confound: "community acceptance patterns may reflect existing nuclear infrastructure rather than rigorous safety evaluation," since both hosts already had nuclear plants and tax revenue from them.

Chain mapping. Schools S2 (devolved authority producing durable decisions) and GenX D2 (a discharge-adjacent approval process with the affected community holding formal power).

Evidence strength. Moderate; behavioral outcome real, attribution confounded by compensation and prior nuclear familiarity.

## 4. Radical open data during emergencies

Maps to: schools S4 ("Share local data from the first weeks"); vaccines V1 ("Share everything, early and openly").

### 4a. Taiwan mask inventory API and g0v mask maps (Taiwan, 2020)

What was tried. During the February 2020 mask shortage, the government published a real-time open API of pharmacy-level mask inventory and worked with the g0v civic tech community, which built public mask maps; one app "got nearly 1 million inquiries on the first day of the ration" (Sidewalk Labs / Jaffe, Medium, March 2020, https://medium.com/sidewalk-talk/how-open-data-and-civic-participation-helped-taiwan-slow-covid-b1449bab5841).

Measured versus claimed outcomes. The fetched account's trust and anxiety claims are explicitly claimed, not measured: the assertion that maps "helped ease people's panic," and the usage-decay reading ("people stopped checking them as much," interpreted as waning anxiety), rest on app analytics plus interpretation, not on any trust or behavior measurement. No study fetched in this sweep measures an effect of the mask data release on trust, compliance, or epidemiological outcomes. Taiwan's aggregate 2020 case counts are heavily confounded (border policy, contact tracing, prior SARS experience), a point the vaccines chain V2 already registers for the Taiwanese comparator.

Chain mapping. Vaccines V1 and schools S4: the state performing the function that NYT/JHU performed by substitution in the US record, with the addition of a civic-tech interface layer.

Evidence strength. Weak for outcomes; Strong as an existence proof that a government can run radical operational transparency in an emergency at national scale within days.

## 5. Standing community processes with binding elements

Maps to: schools S5 ("Give the public a real say before the decision"); also S1.

### 5a. Participatory budgeting in Brazilian municipalities (1989 onward)

What was tried. Municipal PB assigns a share of the capital budget to a standing annual citizen process whose allocations the municipality executes: binding by practice, repeated yearly, at city scale, in hundreds of municipalities.

Measured outcomes. Touchton and Wampler (Comparative Political Studies, 2014, https://scholarworks.boisestate.edu/polsci_facpubs/146/), using an original database of Brazil's largest cities over 20 years, found "PB programs are strongly associated with increases in health care spending," "increases in civil society organizations," and "decreases in infant mortality rates," and that "This connection strengthens dramatically as PB programs remain in place over longer time frames." Gonçalves (World Development, 2014, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/wdevel/v53y2014icp94-110.html) independently "investigates whether the use of participatory budgeting in Brazilian municipalities during 1990-2004 affected the pattern of municipal expenditures and had any impact on living conditions"; her full abstract sits behind ScienceDirect (unfetched), but the published finding, sanitation and health spending shifts followed by infant mortality declines, replicates the Touchton-Wampler direction in an independent design.

Caution. Both are observational panel designs; PB adoption correlates with Workers' Party governance, and the T&W effects strengthen under PT mayors, so partisan bundling is not excluded.

Chain mapping. Schools S5 is the closest fit: a standing, annually recurring, binding-in-practice channel installed upstream of decisions, with measured downstream health outcomes. This is the strongest measured evidence in the entire sweep that a standing binding participation channel moves a hard welfare endpoint.

Evidence strength. Strong (two independent teams, health endpoint), with the partisan confound noted.

### 5b. Ostbelgien permanent Citizens' Council (Belgium, 2019 onward)

What was tried. The Parliament of the German-speaking Community created by decree a permanent Citizens' Council that sets topics and monitors follow-up, plus rotating Citizens' Assemblies; participation in follow-up is "mandatory" for the institution (Deliberative Democracy Digest, https://www.publicdeliberation.net/the-ostbelgien-model-five-years-on/). Six assemblies ran in the first five years, "most of which have gone through the follow-up process foreseen by the Parliament and government."

Measured outcomes. None located. The fetched five-year review is candid: early recommendations "were being proposed already to a certain degree or were deemed too vague" to convert into legislation, and "Institutionalised forms of citizen deliberation remain in their infancy." No trust, satisfaction, or policy-outcome measurement was found.

Chain mapping. Schools S5 (the institutional form the proposal names), with the caveat that the model's own reviewers report design iteration, not measured effects.

Evidence strength. Weak on outcomes; Strong as an existence proof of durable institutionalization (it has survived multiple electoral cycles).

### 5c. Civilian police review boards (United States, 1970s onward)

What was tried. Standing citizen bodies overseeing a coercive public institution: investigation-focused, review-focused, and auditor models; per the Council on Criminal Justice assessment (https://policing.counciloncj.org/assessing-the-evidence/xi-civilian-oversight), "it is rare for an oversight board to have disciplinary authority."

Measured outcomes, largely null or mixed. The CCJ assessment states that "rigorous empirical research on the impact of civilian oversight...does not exist," that complaint-sustain findings conflict across studies, and that for community trust "no research has confirmed this relationship." A 2019 quasi-experimental study linked broad-authority boards to "reduction in racial disparity in disorderly conduct arrests and police homicides." A preregistered survey experiment (PNAS Nexus, 2025, N=2,503, https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/4/9/pgaf267/8238990) found "no statistically significant evidence that the presence of a CRB increases aggregate perceptions of procedural legitimacy," and that when a review board disagreed with the chief, perceived police legitimacy fell (-0.35 to -0.41 points) while agreement produced no significant gain; "individuals tend to side with whichever actor's determination matches their preferred outcomes."

Chain mapping. Schools S5 and vaccines V5, as the field's most important negative comparable: a standing oversight body without binding authority did not measurably raise legitimacy in the one randomized test found, and visible disagreement between the citizen body and the institution reduced it. This bears directly on the project's premise that a standing channel repairs trust; the evidence here says authority-less standing channels may not, and may cut the other way when they conflict with the agency.

Evidence strength. Strong (for the null and the backfire, experimentally measured); Moderate for the field-outcome studies.

### 5d. Oregon Citizens' Initiative Review (United States, 2010 onward)

What was tried. A statutory standing mini-public: 20-24 stratified randomly selected citizens deliberate five days on a ballot measure and publish a Citizens' Statement in the official Voters' Pamphlet reaching over 80% of voters. The statement's placement is binding (it must be printed); the content is advisory to voters.

Measured outcomes. Knobloch, Barthel, and Gastil (Political Studies, 2020, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0032321719852254), using a two-wave 2010 panel (N=971) and a 2012 cross-section (N=1,539), found reading statements increased "issue-specific knowledge for some voters"; "Awareness of the CIR had no effect on internal efficacy but did cause a significant increase in external efficacy"; and "Reading the CIR Statements had its lone significant effect on internal efficacy." The internal-efficacy null for mere awareness is a genuine partial null.

Chain mapping. Schools S5 and S4: a standing process installed upstream of a binding public decision, with measured voter-side effects including a trust-adjacent one (external efficacy, the belief that officials listen).

Evidence strength. Strong for measured voter knowledge and efficacy effects; no downstream policy or welfare endpoint.

## 6. Deliberation before coercive public-health measures

Maps to: vaccines chain V5 ("Public deliberation before any mandate").

### 6a. French citizen consultation on vaccination and the 2018 mandate extension (France, 2016-2018)

What was tried. Facing high vaccine hesitancy, France ran a 2016 Citizen Consultation on Vaccination (steering committee, citizen jury, health professional jury, web consultation) whose steering committee recommended extending infant mandates. Parliament followed: "On 4 December 2017, French parliamentarians passed a law extending the vaccination mandates for children up to 2 years of age from three vaccinations (against diphtheria, tetanus and poliomyelitis) to 11," applying to "all children born after 1 January 2018," enforced by service exclusion rather than fines: "non-vaccinated children will not be admitted to any collective child services such as nurseries or schools" (Lévy-Bruhl et al., Eurosurveillance, 2018, https://www.eurosurveillance.org/content/10.2807/1560-7917.ES.2018.23.17.18-00048).

Measured outcomes. This is the one located real-world case of a national deliberative-consultative process convened before a vaccine mandate, exactly the sequence the V5 proposal specifies. Post-mandate coverage studies exist (Cohen et al., "Impact of mandatory vaccination extension on infant vaccine coverages: Promising preliminary results," Med Mal Infect 2019; Ouldali et al. on meningococcal C, 2022) but all were unfetched in this sweep (PubMed and PMC blocked), so no coverage numbers are reported here. The critical literature (Ward et al., "France's citizen consultation on vaccination and the challenges of participatory democracy in health," Social Science & Medicine 2019, unfetched) argues the consultation's citizen jury did not itself recommend extension and the steering committee's synthesis drove the outcome, so whether this counts as deliberation legitimating a mandate or as expert synthesis wearing deliberative clothing is contested.

Evidence strength. Moderate: the sequence (consultation, then mandate, then measured coverage) is documented; the causal contribution of the deliberative element is contested, and the coverage numbers are unfetched.

### 6b. Calgary fluoridation: council removal, measured harm, plebiscite reinstatement (Canada, 2011-2025)

What was tried. Direct democracy and representative decision on a coercive population-level health measure, run in both directions. "City council votes to discontinue fluoridation" in 2011; a 2021 plebiscite ran the question to voters: "A plebiscite sees 62 per cent support for reinstating fluoride," and fluoride returned to the water June 30, 2025 (Oral Health Group, 2025, https://www.oralhealthgroup.com/dental-governance-regulations/calgarys-fluoride-reinstatement-serves-as-cautionary-tale-for-u-s-quebec-1003987839/).

Measured outcomes. A controlled-comparison cohort study of Grade 2 schoolchildren seven years post-cessation (McLaren et al., published open-access at PMC, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9663766/) measured primary-tooth caries prevalence of "64.8 (62.3-67.3)" percent in Calgary (cessation) versus "55.1 (52.3-57.8)" percent in Edmonton (continued fluoridation). The health endpoint moved measurably in the direction fluoridation science predicts, and the electorate then reversed the decision with the evidence in hand.

Chain mapping. Vaccines V5: a full cycle in which a public decision process removed a coercive measure, the harm was measured, and a second public decision process restored it. It supports a subtler claim than "deliberation improves decisions": public decision channels plus measured outcomes are self-correcting on a roughly decade timescale, and the correction cost a decade of excess childhood caries.

Evidence strength. Strong (measured health endpoint with a comparison city; measured vote).

Also relevant here: the Korea reactor case (1c), where deliberation preceded and reversed a coercive suspension.

## 7. Community co-design of service delivery

Maps to: vaccines chain F2 ("Let communities design how vaccines reach them") and V4.

### 7a. Community-based monitoring of primary health care, Uganda (2004-2005)

What was tried. Björkman and Svensson (Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2009, https://www.jakobsvensson.com/uploads/9/9/1/0/99107788/124-2-735.pdf) randomized 50 rural dispensary catchments: treatment communities received report cards on their local facility, facilitated village meetings (~150 participants), provider meetings, and joint interface meetings producing "community contracts" specifying what the community and staff would each do. The community, not the ministry, set the monitoring priorities: co-design of service accountability rather than of the service itself.

Measured outcomes. "a 33 percent reduction in under-5 mortality" (90% CI 8%-64%, roughly 550 averted deaths in treatment areas in 2005); "Utilization for general outpatient services was 20 percent higher in the treatment compared to control facilities"; infant weight-for-age improved 0.14 z. This is the cleanest causal chain in the sweep from a designed community-voice instrument to a mortality endpoint.

Caution. A follow-up literature (Raffler, Posner, and Parkerson's larger replication, unfetched) found much weaker effects, suggesting context sensitivity; the original's wide confidence interval says the same.

Chain mapping. Vaccines F2 and F3: it demonstrates the mechanism the F2 proposal relies on (community knowledge steering delivery raises hard health outcomes) with experimental identification the COVID record lacks.

Evidence strength. Strong, with the replication caveat.

### 7b. Community-Led Ebola Action / Social Mobilization Action Consortium, Sierra Leone (2014-2015)

What was tried. During the Ebola epidemic, SMAC deployed "nearly 2,500 community mobilizers" across "all 14 districts," reaching over 12,000 communities; communities produced their own action plans and the program "engaged 2,113,902 community members" with structured feedback loops to responders (bioRxiv preprint on community engagement during outbreak response, https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/661959v1.full.pdf).

Measured outcomes. The program measured the "Fraction of cases referred to a health facility with 24 hours" and the "Fraction of safe burials following deaths," plus community action plan progress (85% "in progress," 7% "achieved"). The fetched preprint reports the indicators but the specific before-after percentages did not come through the fetch, and the authors state "Data was self-reported by communities and collected by community mobilizers which may have resulted in reporting bias." The Lancet Planetary Health analysis of SMAC community feedback (2020) was unfetched (403). No experimental control exists; the epidemic was ending as coverage scaled.

Chain mapping. Vaccines V4/F2 (community-designed delivery and norm change during an emergency) and schools S4 (feedback loops as trust infrastructure).

Evidence strength. Moderate at best: enormous documented reach, behavioral indicators tracked, causal attribution unavailable and self-report bias acknowledged.

## 8. Built-in program evaluation

Maps to: vaccines chain F3 ("Fund evaluation alongside the programs").

### 8a. Progresa / Oportunidades conditional cash transfers, Mexico (1997 onward)

What was tried. Progresa built evaluation into its rollout from inception: randomized phased entry across communities, an external evaluator (IFPRI) contracted by the government, and public release of results. A government case study (India DMEO, https://dmeo.gov.in/sites/default/files/2021-08/Package2_WCD_CaseStudy19.pdf) describes the design as "randomized control trials; multiple observations of the same set of families before and after the interventions; and rigorous analysis of the results," with measured impacts including consumption up 22 percent, child malnutrition down 17.2 percent, and secondary enrollment up 11 percent for girls and 7.5 percent for boys, and notes the model informed "similar programmes in Asia, Africa and Latin America and the Caribbean."

Measured outcomes of the evaluation itself. Behrman (World Development, 2010, abstract via RePEc, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/wdevel/v38y2010i10p1473-1485.html) assesses the evaluation as an intervention and concludes "estimated benefit-cost ratios of IFPRI's evaluation of PROGESA considerably exceed one" [spelling in original]. The program's survival across the 2000 change of government (renamed Oportunidades under an opposition administration) is standardly credited in part to the public evidence base; the fetched sources support this as an institutional narrative rather than a measured counterfactual, and Faulkner (Evaluation, 2014, unfetched) argues the RCT's exemplar status is partly constructed.

Chain mapping. Vaccines F3 directly: this is the canonical existence proof that evaluation funded and designed alongside delivery produces effect estimates, survives politics, and exports evidence, the exact inheritance the F3 gap paragraph says the $1.1B COVID equity spend failed to leave.

Evidence strength. Strong for the built-in evaluation producing measured program effects; Moderate for the political-survival claim.

## 9. Protected worker disclosure channels

Maps to: GenX chain D1 ("Give workers a protected way to report").

### 9a. NASA Aviation Safety Reporting System (United States, 1976 onward)

What was tried. A standing confidential reporting channel for aviation workers, run by a third party (NASA, not the regulator FAA), with identity protection and limited immunity from enforcement, "the world's largest confidential voluntary aviation reporting system" (FAA Safety presentation, https://www.faasafety.gov/files/events/EA/EA23/2010/EA2334954/NASA_Reporting.pdf).

Measured outcomes. Scale and throughput are measured: over 500,000 reports by 2001, exceeding 558,000 a year later, and "The ASRS has issued approximately 4,000 alerting messages of all types to the FAA and aviation community authorities since the program's inception," plus 57 published studies. The fetched source itself concedes the outcome gap: it asserts prevention value but "provides limited quantified proof," and no fetched material ties ASRS causally to the measured decline in US aviation fatalities (which is confounded with technology and regulation).

Chain mapping. GenX D1: the design answer to the Purdy problem. The load-bearing design features are third-party receipt (not the employer, not the enforcement agency) and routinized immunity, which converted disclosure from an act of individual sacrifice into a normal professional behavior at half-a-million-report scale.

Evidence strength. Moderate: throughput and alert outputs directly measured; hazard-reduction endpoint unmeasured.

### 9b. OSHA Whistleblower Protection Program (United States)

What was tried. The statutory retaliation-complaint channel covering environmental and safety disclosure, the nearest existing US instrument to the D1 proposal.

Measured outcomes, negative. GAO (GAO-10-722, 2010, https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-10-722) found "OSHA has done little to ensure that investigators have the necessary training and equipment to do their jobs, and that it lacks sufficient internal controls to ensure that the whistleblower program operates as intended," with "differing standards used to screen out complaints" across regions and a national office that "lacks mechanisms, such as access to accurate data and actual case files, to monitor compliance with policies and procedures." The program cannot measure its own outcomes; that is the audit finding.

Chain mapping. GenX D1, as the negative baseline: a protection-on-paper channel without institutional capacity, against which ASRS's third-party design contrasts.

Evidence strength. Strong as measured institutional failure (a federal audit), which functions as a null result for the naive version of the proposal.

## 10. Community environmental monitoring and participatory science

Maps to: GenX chain D1 ("Let residents report early warning signs") and R5.

### 10a. Tonawanda, New York: citizen bucket sampling to an 86% benzene reduction (2004-2013)

What was tried. Residents near Tonawanda Coke, including Jackie James-Creedon, "used homemade bucket set-ups to monitor local air quality," found "elevated concentrations of benzene and formaldehyde," and founded the Clean Air Coalition of Western New York, which pushed the state to run formal monitoring (Global Nonviolent Action Database, Swarthmore, https://nvdatabase.swarthmore.edu/content/clean-air-coalition-western-new-york-hold-tonawanda-coke-accountable-air-pollution-2005-2009). A whistleblowing former employee reported "the plant's habit of performing dirtier processing at night." Tonawanda Coke "faced 19 charges of violating the federal Clean Air Act" and was "found guilty of 14 of the 19 charges" (2013), one of the first federal criminal Clean Air Act convictions of its kind.

Measured outcomes. The NY DEC follow-up (Tonawanda Community Air Quality Study fact sheet, October 2011, https://extapps.dec.ny.gov/docs/air_pdf/tonfact1011.pdf) measured the endpoint: "The results at the industrial monitor (Grand Island Boulevard) show a reduction in benzene concentration of 86%. The results at the residential monitor (Brookside Terrace) show a reduction in benzene concentration of 68%," with the benzene excess-cancer-risk estimate at the industrial monitor falling from "75 in-one-million" to "11 in-one-million."

Chain mapping. GenX D1 (resident-initiated detection where institutions were silent) and R5 (the organized citizen-led sampling the Cape Fear record lacks). This is the best-measured full arc in the family: citizen sampling, state confirmation, criminal enforcement, and a measured ambient-concentration decline.

Evidence strength. Strong. The 86% decline is a directly measured environmental endpoint; attribution is shared between the citizen campaign, the state study, and enforcement, which the chain framing can accommodate since the citizen sampling initiated the sequence.

### 10b. Flint resident water sampling with Virginia Tech (2015)

What was tried. Residents self-collected tap samples using kits from the Virginia Tech Flint Water Study team: "Flint residents returned an astonishing 84% of the sample kits we sent out (252 out of 300 samples)" (flintwaterstudy.org, 2015, https://flintwaterstudy.org/information-for-flint-residents/results-for-citizen-testing-for-lead-300-kits/).

Measured outcomes. The citizen-collected dataset itself is the measurement: "the 90th percentile lead level is currently 25.2 ppb, which is over the EPA standard applied to homes with 'worst case' lead plumbing"; 16.7% of samples exceeded the 15 ppb action level, with a maximum first-draw of 158 ppb. The team concluded "FLINT HAS A VERY SERIOUS LEAD IN WATER PROBLEM." This resident dataset contradicted official sampling and preceded the January 2016 federal emergency declaration.

Chain mapping. GenX D3/D4: participatory science performing the disclosure function that agencies withheld, the same structural position the StarNews story occupies in the GenX chain, but with residents as the sampling instrument rather than a reporter reading a database.

Evidence strength. Strong for detection and disclosure (the citizen data were later validated); downstream health improvement is not attributable to the sampling alone.

## 11. Statutory right-to-know triggers

Maps to: GenX chain D2 ("Warn the public when discharge is approved") and D3 ("A law that forces public notice on detection").

### 11a. EPCRA and the Toxics Release Inventory (United States, 1986 onward)

What was tried. Post-Bhopal, Congress created mandatory annual public disclosure of facility-level toxic releases, the largest standing right-to-know instrument in the domain.

Measured outcomes. EPA's own analysis (Teitelbaum, EPA, https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-09/documents/teitelbaum.pdf) reports "On-site air releases of TRI-reportable chemicals have decreased by 52%, or 838 million pounds, from 2003 to 2013" and hazardous air pollutant stack emissions down 68% over the decade, while attributing declines to treatment, controls, and fuel switching rather than to disclosure per se, and noting quantities "are self-reported by facilities using readily-available data." The key causal study, Konar and Cohen (JEEM, 1997), finding that firms with the largest TRI-day stock price declines subsequently cut emissions most, is unfetched (both ScienceDirect and RePEc withheld the abstract), so its numbers are not reported here. The self-reporting caveat matters: part of early TRI "decline" is paper reduction.

Chain mapping. GenX D2/D3: the template for a notification duty attached to regulated activity. Its documented weakness also matters for the chains: TRI never covered GenX-type PFAS during the relevant years, so the instrument's coverage rules, not its disclosure mechanics, were the binding constraint in the Cape Fear case.

Evidence strength. Moderate: large measured declines in the disclosed quantity; causal attribution to disclosure rests on the unfetched literature; self-report bias documented.

### 11b. Mandated mailing of drinking water quality reports (United States, 1998 onward)

What was tried. The 1996 Safe Drinking Water Act amendments required Consumer Confidence Reports; suppliers over 10,000 customers had to mail them directly to every household, smaller ones only to compile on request, creating a clean policy discontinuity.

Measured outcomes. Bennear and Olmstead (JEEM, 2008; summarized at Resources for the Future, https://www.resources.org/common-resources/information-disclosure-and-drinking-water-quality/) exploited the threshold in Massachusetts: "Mailing CCRs reduced total violations for this group by between 30 and 44 percent, and reduced more serious health violations by 40 to 57 percent," from a baseline where "large water suppliers violated the SDWA about once every two years before 1998." The authors read the mechanism as political: results "are consistent with the hypothesis that the political mechanism is at work."

Chain mapping. GenX D2/D3 directly, and in the same medium (drinking water): a statutory push-notification duty measurably improved regulated behavior. This is the strongest causal evidence located for the project's right-to-know proposals, and it is specifically about pushed disclosure versus available-on-request disclosure, the exact distinction between UCMR3 (available, unread) and the notification trigger the D3 proposal designs.

Evidence strength. Strong.

## 12. Publicly appointed independent science review

Maps to: GenX chain R1 ("Let the public choose experts to review the evidence").

### 12a. The C8 Science Panel (West Virginia / Ohio, 2005-2012)

What was tried. The Leach v. DuPont class settlement created a three-epidemiologist panel, "chosen jointly by the attorneys representing the residents and lawyers representing DuPont" (Hill, Peterson, Carper, Bee & Deitzler, https://www.hpcbd.com/personal-injury/dupont-c8/the-science-panel/): not government-appointed, not company-appointed, but bilaterally appointed with the exposed class holding equal say, the closest existing analog to "the public appoints its own experts." The companion C8 Health Project "gathered information through interviews and questionnaires and collected blood samples from about 69,000 people living near the Washington Works plant" (2005-2006), an enrollment rate achieved by paying class members to participate.

Measured outcomes. The panel delivered probable-link findings that "C8 can cause kidney cancer, testicular cancer, ulcerative colitis, thyroid disease, pregnancy induced hypertension, and medically diagnosed high cholesterol," with a binding consequence: "DuPont is precluded from denying that C8 can cause the six linked disease in any trials that arise from claims made by Class Members," plus DuPont-funded medical monitoring. The panel's findings became the evidentiary basis for later PFOA regulation (as the GenX chain's D1 link already notes).

Chain mapping. GenX R1 directly. The design detail the R1 proposal needs is the appointment rule (joint approval by both sides) and the pre-commitment (findings carry automatic legal consequence), which distinguish it from ordinary agency science advisory boards.

Evidence strength. Strong for mechanism and for binding consequence; the panel's epidemiology is itself the measured outcome. No study measures whether bilateral appointment produced more community trust than an agency panel would have; that comparison does not exist.

## 13. Federally funded but locally designed enforcement and delivery

Maps to: GenX chain R6 ("Federal funding, enforcement designed locally"); vaccines F3.

### 13a. CDC REACH (United States, 1999 onward)

What was tried. Federal grants to community coalitions ("community organizations, universities, local health departments, tribal organizations, and states") that "plan and carry out local, culturally appropriate programs" against chronic disease disparities (Trust for America's Health, https://www.tfah.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/FY22_REACH_Fnl.pdf): federal money, local design, the R6 template applied to health rather than enforcement.

Measured outcomes. Mostly reach, echoing the vaccines chain's F3 diagnosis: "Over 2.9 million people have better access to healthy foods and beverages," "Over 322,000 people have benefited from smoke-free and tobacco-free interventions" (2014-2018 cycle). One fetched outcome finding: implementation "in three Southeast Asian communities (two Vietnamese and one Cambodian) in the United States was associated with decreased rates of smoking among Vietnamese and Cambodian men" (CDC MMWR Supplement, 2014, https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/su6301a2.htm). The frequently cited REACH finding that influenza vaccination disparities narrowed or closed among REACH communities' older adults appears in the secondary literature but was not fetched in this sweep and is not quoted. REACH also carried COVID vaccine confidence supplements, unevaluated in anything fetched here.

Chain mapping. GenX R6 and vaccines F3. REACH is simultaneously the best example of the funding architecture the R6 proposal wants and a live demonstration of the F3 problem: two decades of federal-local programs whose public record is dominated by reach counts.

Evidence strength. Moderate for the smoking finding; Weak for the family's general claim. No instance of federally funded, locally designed environmental *enforcement* with measured outcomes was located in this sweep; the closest analogs are the EPA fenceline monitoring rule influenced by bucket-brigade data (already in the GenX chain via Yale Climate Connections) and state revolving funds, neither of which matches the proposal's local-design-of-enforcement element.

## Cross-cutting observations for the lead researcher

1. Measured hard endpoints cluster in non-deliberative instruments. Infant mortality (PB Brazil, observational), under-5 mortality (Uganda, experimental), benzene (Tonawanda), water violations (CCR mailing), caries (Calgary). The deliberative family's measured outcomes are process and attitude measures (Ireland, Oregon) or policy-adoption counts (France CCC, Korea). This mirrors the project's own finding inside the vaccines chain (access instruments measured, trust instruments not).

2. The best negative results found: (a) civilian review boards, experimental null on legitimacy plus a measured backfire under visible disagreement; (b) decentralization delaying containment across 93 countries; (c) GM Nation's self-selection and cynicism; (d) GAO's audit of OSHA whistleblower protection; (e) the internal-efficacy null for CIR awareness. Items (a) and (e) bear most directly on the project's trust-repair premises.

3. Two design features recur in the positive cases: a pre-commitment that makes the output consequential (Korea's accepted verdict, C8's preclusion clause, CCR's mailing mandate, PB's executed budget share), and a third party insulating the channel (NASA not FAA, IFPRI not SEDESOL, jointly appointed epidemiologists). Instances lacking both (GM Nation, Ostbelgien so far, REACH) show weak or no measured outcomes.

## Questions for the lead researcher

1. The Korea Shin-Gori case is the closest match to "deliberation before a coercive decision with an honored pre-commitment," but the coercion ran opposite to the vaccine case: the deliberation authorized continuing a project the government wanted to stop. Should it attach to V5 as supporting evidence, or does the direction of coercion matter enough to file it as analogical only?

2. Civilian review boards produced an experimental null on procedural legitimacy and a measured backfire when the citizen body disagreed with the institution. Should this be attached to schools S5 as counter-evidence against the standing-channel proposal, or treated as inapplicable because the boards lack the binding authority the proposal specifies? The answer changes whether the proposal's "binding elements" clause is doing all the work.

3. The Publius decentralization finding (delayed containment) concerns the emergency containment phase, while the schools S2 proposal concerns the reopening phase. Is a phase mismatch of this kind disqualifying, or should it enter as the best available measured evidence on confederal crisis decision-making with the mismatch flagged?

4. Bennear and Olmstead measure pushed disclosure to households, while the D3 proposal specifies a trigger firing on regulator receipt of a peer-reviewed detection. The mechanisms differ (routine annual mailing versus event-triggered notice). Close enough to borrow the effect size's direction, or evidence for a sibling instrument only?

5. Participatory budgeting's infant mortality results are the strongest standing-binding-channel outcomes found, but they come from capital budget allocation, far from any of the three chains' domains. Which link should carry them: schools S5 (standing binding channel), vaccines F2 (community allocation of delivery resources), or both?

6. The French 2016 vaccination consultation is the only located instance of the exact V5 sequence, but the critical literature (Ward et al., unfetched) disputes that the citizen jury recommended the mandate at all. Is it worth a follow-up fetch of Ward et al. and the post-mandate coverage studies before citing it in the artifact, given the sweep could not fetch them?

7. For the minipublic-with-experts family, the only measured outcome found is the Suiter et al. cognitive-complexity result, a process measure. Does the project want process-quality measures admitted as "measured outcomes" for this family, or held to the same downstream standard as the other families (in which case this family is nearly empty)?

8. No instance of federally funded, locally designed *enforcement* with measured outcomes was found; REACH is delivery, not enforcement. Should the R6 proposal's comparable be REACH with the mismatch flagged, or should R6 stand with no comparable and an explicit statement that none was found?
